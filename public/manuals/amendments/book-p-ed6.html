Amendment Slip P-6

Book P, fifth edition (September 2026) → sixth edition (September 2026). Issued 10 September 2026. Paste this slip inside the back cover of a fifth-edition copy, or draw a new copy from the Library.

The shed roads have been lengthened. As built, the three roads were 70 m each from their ladder switches; but a ladder staggers the starts of its roads by a lead's length apiece, so roads of one length end at three different places, only the first of them reached the building, and the cars stabled with their ends over the ladder. The Engineer has run all three roads to one far wall, 140 m beyond the headshunt's buffer stop: road 1 is now 150 m, road 2 120 m and road 3 90 m, the buffer stops stand together at shed 0.181, the building covers the far 62 m of every road, and the cars stable inside it, from the buffer stops outwards, well clear of the switches. Nothing else about the shed has changed, and Rule numbers P 10 to P 14 are unchanged.

§ 2.2 Ashgrove Shed Text, Fig. 2.1 and table amended

Why: the roads were too short for the ladder's stagger, and the cars stood over the switches.

"…where road 1 begins and runs 70 m straight to its stop … The three roads lie parallel at 5 m centres, the spacing of the loops, and are 70 m long each: room for three Class 1 cars, or a locomotive and two coaches, with the doors clear of the switch." Buffer stops at shed 0.100, 0.131 and 0.161; the building over the far 40 m of road 1 and the corresponding lengths of roads 2 and 3, 100 m by 16 m. Fig. 2.1 drew three roads of one length ending in echelon, the building over their far ends; the table gave each road as 70 m with its own buffer-stop kilometre.

Road 1 runs 150 m straight from AG T to its stop at the far wall, road 2 120 m from AG U, road 3 90 m from its lead; all three end together at one wall, 140 m beyond the headshunt's buffer stop, "a ladder staggers the starts of its roads, so the roads are of three lengths and the ends are level." Each has room under the roof for two Class 1 cars, or a locomotive and two coaches, and the cars stable from the buffer stops outwards, well clear of the ladder. Buffer stops together at shed 0.181 (0.1805, 0.1810 and 0.1815 to the metre); the building over the far 62 m of all three roads, from shed 0.119 to the wall, 62 m by 16 m. Fig. 2.1 redrawn: the roads to the common wall, their lengths on their labels, the buffer stops in a line, the building's dashed outline over their far ends, the shed kilometre 0.181 at the wall; the caption says so. The table gives road 1 as shed 0.030–0.181, 150 m, buffer stop 0.1805; road 2 as 0.061–0.181, 120 m, 0.1810; road 3 as 0.091–0.181, 90 m, 0.1815; the building as shed 0.119–0.181 on every road, 62 m × 16 m, the cars stabling under the roof from the buffer stops outwards.
