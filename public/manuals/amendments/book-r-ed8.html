The eighth edition follows the working timetable through a whole day. Ashgrove has a shed now, with three roads off the headshunt, and the cars come out of it at dawn and go back at dusk; a car runs empty to Coldwater before its first passengers; drivers work to diagrams. Two kinds of movement that the Book had not named needed a rule: the empty train, which is a train in everything but its passengers, and the shunt, which never leaves station limits and moves on ground signals alone. Rule R 26 gives them one, and says what decides which a movement is: the signal it last passed. Rule R 10 says in a sentence that shunting speed follows the movement and not the vehicle. No existing rule changes its text or its number.

§ 2 Terms and directions Glossary

A shunting move is now something a Class 1 car does, and three new words come with the day's working.

Shunting move. Any movement within station limits that is not a train movement: drawing forward, setting back, running round, coupling. Shunting moves are governed by ground signals and by the station master's instructions, and never exceed 15 km/h.

Duty. … Duties 101 to 401 are driven, Duties 501 and 502 are worked from Wending Box.

Shunting move. Any movement within station limits that is not a train movement: drawing forward, setting back, running round, coupling, a car to or from the shed. Shunting moves are governed by ground signals and subsidiaries and by the station master's instructions, and never exceed 15 km/h. What makes a movement a shunting move is the signal it last passed (Rule R 26).

§ 4 Rule R 10 Speeds Sentence added

The table already said 15 km/h for a shunting move; it did not say that a passenger car shunting is a shunting move.

After the warning-distance paragraph: "Shunting speed applies to any vehicle running as a shunting move, a Class 1 car going to or from the shed included; what makes a movement a shunting move, and when it becomes a train again, is Rule R 26."

§ 5.6 Empty trains and shunts Section added · Rule added

The day's working has movements that carry no passengers, and movements that never leave the station; each needed to know which rules were its own.

A new section closes the station-working chapter, with Rule R 26 Empty trains and shunts: "A train carrying no passengers (an empty train, E-numbered in the working) runs under the same signals, warrants, speeds and stop boards as a passenger train, its doors kept closed. A shunt within station limits, between a platform and the shed or the headshunt, moves on ground signals and subsidiaries at shunting speed, needs neither warrant nor baton, and stops short of any vehicle in the road it enters. What a movement is, the signal it last passed decides: a main aspect makes it a train; a ground signal or a subsidiary at SHUNT makes it a shunting move; a vehicle in a shed is a shunting move until it leaves under a main aspect. Stop boards do not apply to shunting moves, and ground signals do not apply to trains."

The section walks a car out of Ashgrove Shed and back again to show where the rule's two halves begin and end, and its "why" box explains why a shunt needs no warrant (it never enters a block section, and the whole of its road is the station master's to see) and why the aspect decides (it is the one thing the driver and the Box are both looking at).

§ 9.1 The working timetable Paragraph added
