Amendment Slip P-4

Book P, third edition (September 2026) → fourth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste this slip inside the back cover of a third-edition copy, or draw a new copy from the Library.

The Railway has a second line. The Fernhollow branch leaves the main line at a junction switch beyond Wending's north switch, curves away through 25 degrees, and climbs a side valley at 10‰ to a single platform at Fernhollow. This edition describes the junction, the branch and its kilometrage, adds the branch's gradient diagram and its two posts, extends the speed and warning-distance table, and fixes block section C. Laying the junction also moved Wending's north station limit, its home signal WD 8, its distant and its advance board 120 m outwards, and the figures for those are corrected wherever they appear. Rule numbers P 10 to P 14 are unchanged.

§ 2 The line Rewritten

Why: the Railway is no longer one line and three stations, and Wending's north end has moved.

"One line, three stations, six kilometres and a little more." The main line was straight "for the whole of this edition"; Wending's station limits ran 2.850–3.400 with "a stub of single line beyond each switch"; plain line section B began at 3.400; kilometrage was described for the main line only. Five rows in the table of places.

"One line and a branch, four stations, eight kilometres and a little more." The main line is straight for the whole of its length, and the Railway's only curve is where the branch leaves it. The branch is electrified with the rest. The branch keeps its own count, lettered F. Wending's row now runs 2.850–3.500 and names the north stub (3.220–3.360) and the junction switch WD J at 3.360; section B begins at 3.500; two rows are added, the Fernhollow branch, section C (F 0.000–2.000, 40 km/h, the junction curve, the 10‰ climb, Wending's branch limit ending at F 0.140) and Fernhollow (F 2.000–2.320, one platform track from F 2.060, platform F 2.100–2.260, no switches, buffer stop at F 2.320).

§ 2.1 The junction and the branch New section

Why: the junction and the branch's kilometrage are the Engineer's to define, and every other Book quotes them.

No such section.

The junction switch WD J at km 3.360, 140 m beyond the north switch: a turnout that chooses between two lines, its toe facing the station, laid on the level inside Wending's limits, with a switch indicator like every other switch. The branch curves away through 25 degrees over its first 100 m, a curve of about 230 m radius, then runs straight up the side valley for 2.2 km to Fernhollow. Branch kilometrage F 0.000 at WD J to F 2.320 at the buffer stop, hectometre and kilometre posts lettered F, positions quoted with the letter, Down on the branch away from Wending. The branch is built to the standards of § 1.1 but for a line speed of 40 km/h and a check rail through the curve. Fernhollow has no loop and no switches. A note explains why the junction lies beyond the station rather than at its throat, and what the Engineer owes for it.

§ 3 The profile Lead amended

Why: the profile chapter now covers two lines.

"…rests through the station, and climbs again to Coldwater."

"…rests through the station, and climbs again to Coldwater; the branch climbs its own side valley to Fernhollow (§ 3.3)."

§ 3.1 Reading a gradient Note amended

Why: the Railway now has a 10‰ grade.

"12‰ is 1 in 83, 8‰ is 1 in 125, 6‰ is 1 in 167."

"12‰ is 1 in 83, 10‰ is 1 in 100, 8‰ is 1 in 125, 6‰ is 1 in 167."

§ 3.2 What a gradient does to a train Paragraph extended

Why: the branch grade sits between the two main-line grades and a driver should know where.

The second paragraph ended with the Up train from Coldwater on the fall for 2.2 km before the level at Wending.

A sentence is added: the branch's 10‰ is between the two, 3.7 kN on a Class 1 car; an Up train from Fernhollow is on it for 1.6 km, and the fall ends 300 m short of the junction, on the level, where the train must be at 25 km/h and ready for WD 10.

§ 3.3 The branch profile New section

Why: the branch has a profile of its own, and it needs its own diagram and table.

No such section; Fig. 3.1 and its table covered the main line only.

A paragraph, Fig. 3.2 and a section table. Fig. 3.2 is the gradient diagram of the branch, F 0 to F 2.32, drawn to the same exaggeration and the same height scale as Fig. 3.1, Wending and WD J at the left, Fernhollow at the right, the gradient posts F 0.3 and F 1.9 marked. The table: F 0.000–0.300 level at 25.2 m; F 0.300–1.900 rising 10‰, 16.0 m, to 41.2 m; F 1.900–2.320 level at 41.2 m. Gradients on the branch are given in the branch's Down direction, towards Fernhollow. The junction, the whole of Wending's branch station limit and Fernhollow itself stand on the level. Fernhollow is 41.2 m above Ashgrove, 16 m above the junction and 1.6 m lower than Coldwater. Down trains climb, Up trains descend, on the branch as on the main line.

§ 4 Gradient posts Amended

Why: two more posts stand on the ground.

"…written on five small posts, one at each place where the slope changes." Five rows in the table of posts, 0.5 to 5.6. The side note covered the main line. "A driver who knows the five posts knows the whole line."

"…written on seven small posts, one at each place where the slope changes, five on the main line and two on the branch." Two rows added: F 0.3, level to 10‰ rising, and F 1.9, 10‰ rising to level, each read in both directions. The side note adds that on the branch "Wending side" is the Up side and "Fernhollow side" the Down side, and that the branch posts carry the letter F so that F 0.3 is not mistaken for 0.3. "A driver who knows the seven posts knows the whole Railway."

§ 5 Speeds and warning distances Amended

Why: Wending's north end moved with the junction, and the branch has four new places where a speed changes.

Up into Wending: advance 3.600, board 3.400, WD 8D 3.520, WD 8 3.320. Down leaving Wending: board 3.400 · 50. No branch rows. One note, on the 8‰ Up approach.

The Up-into-Wending row, now "from Coldwater", reads advance 3.700, board 3.500, WD 8D 3.640, WD 8 3.440, 200 m. Down leaving Wending for Coldwater: 3.500 · 50. Four branch rows: Up into Wending from Fernhollow, advance F 0.390 · 25, board F 0.140 · 25, WD 10D at F 0.330, WD 10 at F 0.080, 250 m, the approach falling 10‰ to post F 0.3, the threshold exactly; Down into Fernhollow, advance F 1.800 · 25, board F 2.000 · 25, FH 1D at F 1.830, FH 1 at F 2.030, 200 m, the approach rising 10‰ then level; Down leaving Wending for Fernhollow, F 0.140 · 40; Up leaving Fernhollow, F 2.000 · 40. The text notes that the rule is the same on the branch, where the line speed is 40 km/h, since it is written for the fall and not for the speed. The first note records that Wending's north limit and WD 8 moved out to 3.500 and 3.440 when the junction was laid and the advance board and distant moved with them; a second note explains that the branch's advance board and WD 10D are the first on the Railway to earn the extra fifty metres from a fall of one hundredth.

§ 6 Features of the line Lead amended

Why: there is a fourth feature.

"Three things on the line are neither track nor signal… a gap in the wire, a gate across the formation, and two lengths of line that must be kept clear for the boxes."

"Four things on the line are neither track nor signal… a gap in the wire, a gate across the formation, three lengths of line that must be kept clear for the boxes, and a place where the line divides."

§ 6.3 The block sections Amended

Why: section B begins at the moved WD 8, and section C is new.

"There are two": section A, AG 2 at 0.420 to WD 1 at 2.880, and section B, WD 8 at 3.320 to CW 1 at 5.980. Table rows for sections A and B, the neutral section and Millers' Crossing.

"There are three": section A as before; section B from WD 8 at 3.440 to CW 1 at 5.980; section C, Wending to Fernhollow, from WD 10 at F 0.080 to FH 1 at F 2.030. Sections B and C begin at two different homes on two different lines and meet at the junction, which belongs to neither: the junction and the north stub are Wending's, and the Box locks them for one movement at a time. The table gains Block section C (F 0.080–F 2.030, homes WD 10 and FH 1) and Junction WD J (3.360, marked by its switch indicator and the branch curve F 0.000–0.100, Book S, Rule S 22), and section B's kilometrage is corrected.

§ 6.4 The junction New section

Why: the junction is the one place where the line divides, and it wears faster than anything else on the Railway.

No such section.

WD J lying normal carries the main line to Coldwater, lying reverse turns a train over the branch curve to Fernhollow; facing for Down trains, trailing for Up trains from either line. Its indicator at the toe says which way it lies, and a driver leaving Wending northwards reads it, since WD 7 and WD 9 clear for either destination. The Engineer's interest: every train of two lines passes its blades and crossing nose, the curve begins at its heel, and so it is walked with every length, gauged at toe, heel and nose, and its blades examined for their fit. It is laid on the level and kept on it, so that a stopped portion never has to hold itself against gravity while its other half moves away.

§ 7 Looking after the line Item added

Why: the list of what wears now has a junction and a curve on it.

The list ended with "The crossing and the gap."

A further item, "The junction and the curve": the blades and crossing nose of WD J take every train of two lines, the outer rail of the branch curve takes the side thrust of each one turning, and the check rail through the curve is the first rail on the Railway that will need turning or renewing.

§ 8 Glossary Entries added

Why: three new words, one old one that now covers three sections.

The glossary ended at Block section, "sections A and B" implied.

Block section now names sections A, B and C. New entries: Branch (a line that leaves the main line at a junction and keeps its own kilometrage; the Fernhollow branch, lettered F, from WD J to F 2.320), Junction (the switch at which two lines divide or meet; WD J at km 3.360, facing for Down trains and trailing for Up), and Check rail (a rail laid inside the running rail through a curve; on the branch curve).
