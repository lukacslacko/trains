The timetable gains a third block section, a block of branch train numbers, a chapter on portions with the new Rule T 16, Duty 401 in the working timetable, and a second time–distance diagram. Chapters 5 to 8 of the first edition are now chapters 6 to 9; Rules T 10 to T 14 keep their numbers. The entries below follow the Book's order.

§ 2.1 The car and the train Paragraph added

Why: a train may now divide or be joined, and its number has to know what to do.

A train that divides keeps its number on the main line and gives its branch portion a branch number: train 7 arrives at Wending as two cars and leaves it as train 7 for Coldwater and train 31 for Fernhollow. A train that is joined takes the number of the portion that stood waiting: train 32 from Fernhollow is called on to train 8 at Wending, and one train 8 leaves for Ashgrove. § 5 sets out the rules.

§ 3.3 Dwell Entry added

Why: dividing and joining are dwells of their own kind.

Dividing and joining dwell. Seven minutes at Wending for a train that divides: the calling dwell, the uncoupling, the tail lights on the new rear cab, and two departures two minutes apart; and up to nine for a train that joins, since the standing portion must wait for the arriving one to be called on and coupled, and the brake to be proved. See § 5.

Added: "Sections B and C both begin at Wending's north end, and they share the north stub and the junction switch WD J. The stub and the junction are worked as one route at a time under route locking (Rule S 32): a train leaving for Coldwater, a train leaving for Fernhollow, a train arriving from either, each holds WD J from the clearing of its signal until it has passed clear, and the next may not have the junction until then. § 5 Portions New chapter

Why: a train that divides or is joined needs its own rules of booking; the chapters that followed are renumbered 6 to 9.

5.1 Dividing. Train 7 leaves Ashgrove as one train of two cars, 1002 and 1003, coupled with pipe and control line and worked from the leading cab under Rule D 26. It is one train the whole way to Wending: one number, one warrant, one baton. At Wending it is uncoupled as Rule D 28 describes, and from that moment there are two trains standing on platform 1: train 7, which will go on to Coldwater, and train 31, which will go up the branch to Fernhollow. Each now needs its own line clear, its own warrant, its own cleared starter and its own baton; the box treats them as it would treat two trains that had arrived separately, except that they cannot both leave at once through the same starting signal.

Rule T 16 Portions. A train that divides is one train until it is uncoupled; each portion then has its own departure, baton and warrant. Portions leave a station in the order they stand, the one nearest the exit first.

Why we do this. The order is not always the one you would guess from the destinations. On the way up the valley the Coldwater car leads and leaves first. But a pair that has turned round at Ashgrove comes back with its cars the other way about: train 9 arrives at Wending with the branch car, 1001, at the front. The branch portion, train 33, must therefore leave before the Coldwater portion can, and the timetable books 33 at 10:54 and 9 at 10:56, not the other way round. A timetable that ignored which car stands nearest the starter would book a departure the box could not give.

Note. Two portions leaving through WD 7 in turn need the junction twice: WD J straight for Coldwater, WD J for the branch for Fernhollow. Under route locking (Rule S 32) the second route cannot be set until the first portion has passed clear of the switch, which is why the two departures are booked two minutes apart and not one.

5.2 Joining. Joining is dividing in reverse, and it is booked around the portion that arrives first. The standing portion takes its platform, arrives complete, and waits with its train brake applied; its warrant is cancelled like any other. The arriving portion is not given a clear home: it is called on under the home signal's subsidiary (Rule S 30), which tells its driver that the platform is occupied and to proceed at shunting speed prepared to stop short of the vehicles. It couples to the standing portion at 2 km/h or less, its driver connects the pipe and control line, and the brake is proved from the leading cab. The joined train then leaves under the number of the portion that stood waiting, with one warrant and one baton: train 32 from Fernhollow is called on to train 8 at platform 2, and one train 8 leaves for Ashgrove; train 34 is called on to train 10, and train 10 takes the pair home. The standing portion is always the one booked to arrive first, and it is always given the platform kept for its direction, so that the arriving portion comes in behind it and the joined train's leading cab is the one that has been there all along. If the arriving portion is late, the standing one waits: a joined train cannot leave as half of itself.

5.3 How the timetable shows it. A train that divides has two rows under its number: one for the whole train as far as the station where it divides, and one for the portion that keeps the number beyond it; the other portion has a row of its own under its branch number. Train 7 in § 6.4 is written as 1002+1003 Ashgrove–Wending and then 1002 Wending–Coldwater, and train 31 as 1003 Wending–Fernhollow. A train that is joined has a row for each portion arriving and one row for the whole under the standing portion's number: train 8 is 1004 Coldwater–Wending, then, after train 32 has been called on, 1004+1001 Wending–Ashgrove. The word split or joins in the times column says which.

Why: the sheet now carries branch legs and portions.
