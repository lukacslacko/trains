Amendment Slip I-4

Book I, third edition (September 2026) → fourth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a third-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

Four entries, in Book order. Occasion: every switch now carries an indicator that shows the driver which way it lies, and the Works needs the drawing.

§ 7.3 Switch indicators New section

Why: switches are now drawn as they lie, and a driver setting back through one must be able to see, from any leg, which way it is set; Book S gives the indicator a rule, Book I gives it a drawing.

A new section between the signal name plates and the boards, with Fig. 7.3, reading:

Every switch tells the driver which way it lies. The switch indicator is an Ink box 400 mm square with an Ivory rim 12 mm wide, on a Slate post 1.0 m to the underside of the box, standing at the toe of the switch on the side of the line away from the diverging track. Each of its two faces, one towards the toe and one towards the branches, carries a single White bar 300 mm long and 40 mm wide. The bar lies the way the switch lies: upright when the switch is set for the straight route, leaning 35° from the vertical towards the diverging track when it is set for the diverging route. Both faces move together, so a driver reads the same answer from any leg of the switch. The bar is a lamp, lit at night, and it is White for the reason the ground-signal lamps are White: it is information for a shunting move, and White is the colour the Railway keeps for that. It is not lettering, and the signal name plate of § 7.2 remains the only white lettering on the line.

Fig. 7.3: the indicator's face towards the toe, set straight and set diverging with the diverging track to the right, with the box, the bar length and width and the 35° lean dimensioned in brass, and the note "1.0 m to rail".

The indicator stands where the points begin, 3.4 m from the centre of the track on the side away from the diverging route, so that it is never between the two tracks and never behind a wagon standing on either of them. It shows how the switch lies and nothing else; the ground signal beside it says whether the driver may go. Book S says how the two are read together, and what a driver does when the indicator shows the switch set against the move. The Works makes the bar as one lamp behind a stencil, so that the leaning bar is the same lamp turned, never a second lamp.

The definition list gains: Switch indicator. Ink box with an Ivory rim, two faces, a single White bar on each: upright for the straight route, leaning towards the diverging track for the diverging route. One at the toe of every switch, on the side away from the diverging track.

Rule I 30 Signage Rule amended

Why: the rule must place the indicator and confine it to its one message.

… A gradient post stands at every change of grade and at no other place, its arms tilted to the one pattern and lettered with the grade, never re-tilted to a site. Do not add a sign that is not in Book S, and do not vary a drawing to suit a site; move the post instead.

… A gradient post stands at every change of grade and at no other place, its arms tilted to the one pattern and lettered with the grade, never re-tilted to a site. A switch indicator stands at the toe of every switch, on the side away from the diverging track, and shows the way the switch lies and nothing else. Do not add a sign that is not in Book S, and do not vary a drawing to suit a site; move the post instead.
