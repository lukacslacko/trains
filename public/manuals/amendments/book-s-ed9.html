Amendment Slip S-9

Book S, eighth edition (September 2026) → ninth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of an eighth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The ninth edition adds a chapter. Until now every page of Book S was written for the driver, who reads the signals; with the Railway's first signaller's duties, 501 and 502, a driver may take Wending Box for a morning, and the Book needed a chapter for the person who clears them. The new § 10, Working a box, describes the chair, the block and the exchange of line clear, the lever frame and the setting of the road, the starter and the warrant, the baton shown from the box, train out of section, the register and the working, what the Incident Book records against a box, and the two duties. It carries four new rules, S 34 to S 40. No existing rule changes its number or its meaning; the glossary moves from § 10 to § 11 and gains seven entries. The entries below follow the Book's order.

§ 1 Principles Paragraph added · Wording changed

Why: each edition opens with a note saying what it changed; the eighth edition's note is put in the past, as the seventh's was before it.

The chapter opens with the note "The subsidiary under the head": "This eighth edition changes one drawing and the words around it. A subsidiary is no longer fixed beside the head … No rule changes its number or its meaning. The signal drawings of § 2 and § 4 are the reference drawings from which the other Books copy."

A new note "Working a box" opens the chapter: "This ninth edition adds a chapter for the person in the box. Until now every page of this Book was written for the driver, who reads the signals; § 10 is written for the signaller, who clears them: the exchange of line clear in words (Rule S 34), the cancelling of a warrant when its train has arrived complete (Rule S 36), setting the road before the train reaches it (Rule S 38), and the baton shown from the box at the booked time (Rule S 40). Duties 501 and 502 put a driver in Wending Box for a morning. Nothing said to the driver in the earlier chapters has changed; § 10 is the same rules read from the other end of the telephone." The subsidiary note now reads "The eighth edition changed one drawing … No rule changed its number or its meaning. The signal drawings of § 2 and § 4 remain the reference drawings …"

§ 7.4 Block working and the Line Warrant Sentence added

Why: the block working is now described from both ends of the telephone, and the driver's chapter points to the box's.

The paragraph "What the driver sees of it" ends: "… the warrant says whether it is, and the starter repeats the warrant."

It ends: "… the warrant says whether it is, and the starter repeats the warrant. What the box sees of it, lever by lever and word by word, is § 10."

§ 10 Working a box Chapter added · Rules S 34, S 36, S 38, S 40 added · Fig. 10.1 added

Why: Duties 501 and 502 put the player in Wending Box in place of Mr Pell, and everything the chair asks of them needed writing down where the driver's rules are written.

No chapter. The box's side of block working was described only in § 7.4's exchange, as seen by the driver; the glossary followed § 9 directly.

A chapter of nine sections between § 9 and the glossary. 10.1 The signaller's chair: the box building at the stop-board end of the first platform, what the player takes over, the desk in three parts (block, lever frame, baton) with the working under it and the register beside it, and a note on why the box is a telephone, a frame and a disc. 10.2 The block: line clear, with Rule S 34: the exchange in words, the two conditions for giving line clear, the "Line clear" lever dead with its reason, "Ask Coldwater" for a train of your own, refusals in words, and why asking early is not free. 10.3 The lever frame: setting the road, with Rule S 38: one lever per route under its signal, the four reasons a lever is dead, Fig. 10.1 (the Wending frame, nineteen levers in nine groups, two pulled for the crossing) and a table of the nine signals and their levers; the home set before the train; the other platform; the call-on; two portions and the lock; Replace. 10.4 The starter and the warrant: Rule S 28 from the box's side, and the fixed order of a departure. 10.5 The baton, with Rule S 40: the baton card, the Show baton lever live only under a cleared starter with the doors closed, shown at the time. 10.6 Train out of section, with Rule S 36: arrived complete, the cancelling lever red while the train is still in section, the call-on arrived complete only when coupled. 10.7 The register and the working: the box's duty sheet, the clock brought to two and a half minutes before the next event, the working's advice, the Messages tab as the telephone. 10.8 What the Incident Book records: Warrant cancelled early (S 36), Train held at the home (S 38), Baton shown late (S 40), and what is not recorded. 10.9 Duties 501 and 502: the register of each from Wending Box, and what is new to the chair in the second.

§ 11 Glossary Renumbered · Entries added · Entries amended

Why: the glossary follows the new chapter, and the chair has words of its own.

§ 10 Glossary. Box: "The signal box: the room, the frame, the register and the person working it. Ashgrove Box, Wending Box, Coldwater Box, Fernhollow Box." Frame: "The row of interlocked levers in the box that set switches and clear signals." No entries for Lever frame, Line clear, Register, Replace, Signaller, Train out of section or Working.

§ 11 Glossary. Box gains "A small building at the stop-board end of the station's first platform, on the platform side. Its station master works it; a driver may take it for a duty (§ 10)." Frame gains "See Lever frame for how it is worked." New entries: Lever frame (one lever for each route under its signal, lit when pulled, dead with its reason), Line clear (the exchange in words, Rule S 34), Register (the box's record of the day and its duty sheet), Replace (a cleared signal put back to STOP; the route releases once nothing is approaching), Signaller (the person working a box, signing as the station), Train out of section (the cancelling of a warrant on arrival complete, Rule S 36) and Working (what the timetable expects of a box, with the thing to be done about each movement).
