Amendment Slip S-6

Book S, fifth edition (September 2026) → sixth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a fifth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The sixth edition is the Coldwater extension, and it is the largest change since the first edition. The line now runs beyond Wending to Coldwater at km 6.400; Wending has become a through station with a platform on each loop track and ten signals; Coldwater mirrors Ashgrove. Two trains may be on the line at once, so the line has gained distant signals, subsidiaries under two of Wending's starters, two block sections worked by Line Warrants, a neutral section with section and resume boards, and a whistle board for Millers' Crossing. Rule S 10 is amended; Rules S 24 and S 28 are new; no rule is renumbered. WD 3 is withdrawn. The entries below follow the Book's order; the largest additions are summarised rather than quoted in full.

§ 1 Principles Wording changed

Why: a preface names the edition; the fourth principle now allows main signals to speak to every movement; a ninth principle states the block in one line; § 1.2 gains Coldwater Box and A. Ashby, and the kilometrage note runs to Coldwater.

Fourth principle: "The two kinds of signal look nothing alike, and neither speaks to the other's movements." Boxes: Ashgrove Box and Wending Box; station masters T. Marrow and J. Pell. Kilometrage "to the buffer stop at Wending, km 3.300".

A note "The Coldwater extension" opens the chapter. Fourth principle: "The two kinds of signal look nothing alike. A main signal speaks to every movement; a ground signal speaks only to shunting." After the eight: "To these the Coldwater extension added a ninth, which belongs with the other eight: one warrant, one train, one section." § 1.2 lists Ashgrove, Wending and Coldwater Boxes, the register of Line Warrants, and A. Ashby at Coldwater; messages may be signed "Ashby, Coldwater". Kilometrage counts "to the buffer stop at Coldwater, km 6.400. Wending lies between, km 2.850 to 3.400 within its limits."

§ 2 Main signals Wording changed

Why: main signals now govern shunting movements as well as trains; the platform convention covers Wending's homes; the "why" on distant signals is fulfilled; § 2.3 lists the new homes and starters and introduces the distant.

Lead: "Main signals govern train movements." Note: "The terminal-platform convention … At Ashgrove and Wending the home signals AG 2 and WD 1 therefore never show green." § 2.3: "Ashgrove's home is AG 2; Wending's is WD 1. … Ashgrove's starter is AG 1; Wending's is WD 2. A starting signal is cleared only when the section to the next station has been offered and accepted (§ 7.2)."

Lead: "Main signals govern every movement that reaches them, train or shunting." Note retitled "The platform convention", covering AG 2, WD 1, WD 8 and CW 1, with the through-station case explained. § 2.3: Wending has two homes, WD 1 and WD 8, and four starters, WD 2, WD 4, WD 7, WD 9; Coldwater has CW 1 and CW 2; a starting signal "is cleared only with the Line Warrant for the section ahead in the box's hand (§ 7.4)"; a "Distant signal" entry is added.

Rule S 10 Signals at STOP Wording changed

Why: the subsidiary makes a main signal at STOP mean two things to two kinds of movement.

Never pass a main signal at STOP, and never pass a ground signal at SHUNT STOP. Bring the front of the train to a stand at the post and wait for the aspect to change or for the box to speak. Passing a signal at STOP is a signal passed at danger and is written in the Incident Book whatever the outcome.

Never pass a main signal at STOP, and never pass a ground signal at SHUNT STOP. A main signal at STOP whose subsidiary is lit (§ 2.4) is STOP for a train and SHUNT for a shunting movement. Bring the front of the train to a stand at the post and wait for the aspect to change or for the box to speak. Passing a signal at STOP is a signal passed at danger and is written in the Incident Book whatever the outcome.

§ 2.4 Subsidiaries New section

Why: WD 4 and WD 7 carry a subsidiary shunt aspect in place of a separate ground signal on the same post.

Two of Wending's starting signals, WD 4 on track 2 and WD 7 on track 1, carry a subsidiary: a small black box beside the head with two white lamps, the same lamps as a ground signal. Normally dark; when the box clears a shunt route beginning at that signal, the main head stays at STOP and the white lamps light diagonally, rising to the right: SHUNT, meaning exactly what SHUNT means on a ground signal, and nothing at all to a train. Fig. 2.2 shows WD 7 at STOP with the subsidiary dark and lit. A lit subsidiary is still a main signal at STOP: a train standing at WD 7 with the white lamps lit has not been given the road. WD 3 of the earlier editions is withdrawn; its move is WD 7's subsidiary.

§ 2.5 Distant signals New section

Why: with two trains on the line a home may be at STOP for the other train, and the driver must learn it one warning distance out.

A distant signal stands one warning distance before a home signal and repeats what the home shows: two lamps, green top, amber bottom, on an ivory chevron plate, named for its home with a D (WD 1D). CAUTION: the home ahead may be at STOP, reduce speed so as to stop at it. CLEAR: the home and every main signal through the station are clear. Dark: treat as CAUTION. A distant never shows STOP. It stands 200 m before its home where the approach is level or rising and 250 m where it falls at 10‰ or more: AG 2D at 0.670, WD 1D at 2.680, WD 8D at 3.520, CW 1D at 5.780. Because every home admits its trains to a platform where they stop, every distant shows CAUTION to every stopping train, and means it. Fig. 2.3 shows both aspects, with a "why" on the chevron plate and the missing red lamp.

Rule S 24 Distant signals New rule

Why: the distant needs a rule of its own, because it is the only signal that is never passed at danger.

At a distant signal at CAUTION, reduce speed so as to be able to stop at the home signal it repeats, whatever the home turns out to show. A distant at CLEAR promises a clear run through the station. A distant is never passed "at danger": it gives warning, not instruction, and the instruction is the home's.

§ 3 Ground signals Wording changed

Why: shunting movements now obey main signals too, and pass one at STOP only under its subsidiary.

Lead ends: "They are low, black and unmistakable." § 3.2 note ends: "if your last signal was a main signal, you are a train; if it was a ground signal, you are shunting."

Lead ends: "They are low, black and unmistakable. Shunting movements obey main signals as well; ground signals are the signals that belong to them alone." § 3.2 note ends: "if your last signal was a main signal's amber or green, you are a train; if it was a ground signal or a subsidiary, you are shunting. The other way round is stricter: a shunting movement obeys every main signal it reaches, and passes one at STOP only under its lit subsidiary (§ 2.4). A locomotive running round at Wending leaves track 1 under WD 7's subsidiary, not past WD 7 as if it were not there."

§ 4 Boards, table and § 4.1 Wording changed

Why: three boards are new, and every board's place on the extended line is given.

Fig. 4.1 captioned "The five boards of the present Railway". Table: stop board, Limit of Shunt, speed board, advance speed board, buffer stop, posts, gradient post. § 4.1 placed the stop boards at 0.135 and 3.165, the Limit of Shunt boards at 0.400 and 2.900, the speed boards at 0.450 and 2.850, the advance boards at 0.700 and 2.650, and the gradient posts at 0.500, 2.400 and 2.800.

Fig. 4.1 captioned "The four boards of the first line … The whistle, section and resume boards of the Coldwater extension are drawn in § 4.5." Table gains rows for the whistle board (black W on an ivory disc: one long blast, a crossing ahead), the section board (black square, white bar across: shut off power) and the resume board (white bar along: power may be taken again). § 4.1 now places: stop boards at 0.135, 3.165 (track 1, Down), 3.015 (track 2, Up) and 6.265; Limit of Shunt boards at 0.400, 2.900, 3.280 and 6.000, with the note that Wending has no headshunt and its north stub serves; speed boards at 0.450, 2.850, 3.400 and 5.950; advance boards at 0.700 (250 m), 2.650, 3.600 and 5.750 (200 m); gradient posts at 0.500, 2.400, 2.800, 3.400 and 5.600; whistle boards at 4.500 and 4.900 for the crossing at 4.700; section and resume boards back to back at 4.100 and 4.300.

§ 4.2 Speed boards and advance speed boards Wording changed

Why: the distants share the warning distance, and the posts note covers the new approaches.

Added to the advance-board paragraph: "The distant signals use the same warning distance before their homes (§ 2.5), so at every station the triangle and the chevron stand close together on the approach." Added to the posts note: "North of Wending the Up advance board at 3.600 stands at post 3.6 for the 25 board at 3.400, and the Down advance board at 5.750 between posts 5.7 and 5.8 for the Coldwater 25 board at 5.950."

§ 4.3 Posts and § 4.4 Gradient posts Wording changed

Why: the posts run to km 6.400 and the profile continues to Coldwater.

"…to the end of the line at km 3.300…" · "…and is level again through Wending, which stands 25.2 m above Ashgrove. Down trains climb; Up trains descend."

"…to the end of the line at km 6.400…" · "…and is level through Wending, which stands 25.2 m above Ashgrove. From km 3.400 it climbs again at 8‰ towards the quarries, and from 5.600 it is level into Coldwater, 42.8 m above Ashgrove. Down trains climb; Up trains descend."

§ 4.5 Whistle, section and resume boards New section

Why: the extension crosses a farm track and a boundary between two electrical feeds.

Fig. 4.4 draws the three boards face on. The whistle board, an ivory disc with a black W, stands 200 m before a crossing and asks for one long blast (Rule R 18); the only crossing is Millers' Crossing at km 4.700, a gated farm track. The section board (black square, white bar across the line) stands 90 m before the neutral section at km 4.190–4.210 and says shut off power now; the resume board (white bar along the line) stands 90 m beyond and says power may be taken again; both pairs share posts at 4.100 and 4.300 and face both ways. The driving is Book D, Rule D 24; the electrical reasons are Book P. A note that these boards have nothing to do with the signals.

§ 6 Station layouts, lead, § 6.1 and legend Wording changed

Why: three stations, a through station, distants and subsidiaries need naming.

Lead: "Both stations on the line are laid out the same way, mirrored…" Numbering: codes AG and WD; "Main signals take the lowest numbers, 1 and 2. Ground signals take the rest, 3 to 6." Switches: A on the single-line side, B on the headshunt side.

Lead: the two termini are mirrored, Wending between them is a through station with a platform on each track and a stub at each end. Numbering adds the code CW; at Wending the main signals are 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 9 and the ground signals 5 and 6; "A distant signal takes its home's number with the letter D"; switch A is at the Ashgrove end of a loop and B at the Coldwater end. The legend gains the distant signal and reads "ground signal (or a subsidiary)". The Ashgrove caption and table gain AG 2D at 0.670.

§ 6.3 Wending Rewritten

Why: Wending is now a through station.

Wending "at the head of the valley, at the Down end of the line", with a headshunt and buffer stop at km 3.300; Fig. 6.2 and a table of WD 1 to WD 6 with one stop board and one Limit of Shunt; a note that track 2 has no platform.

Wending "a through station by the weir, halfway up the valley", with a stub at each end and no headshunt; platform 1 on track 1 for Down trains and platform 2 on track 2 for Up trains. Fig. 6.2 redrawn with both platforms, both stubs, both Limits of Shunt, and WD 1, 2, 4 (subsidiary), 5, 6, 7 (subsidiary), 8 and 9. The table runs from the gradient post at 2.400 to the advance board at 3.600 and includes WD 1D at 2.680, the Up stop board at 3.015, the Down stop board at 3.165, the north Limit of Shunt at 3.280, WD 8 at 3.320, the speed boards and gradient post at 3.400 and WD 8D at 3.520. A note "Which platform" explains the platform convention and records that WD 3 is withdrawn.

§ 6.4 Section B: Wending to Coldwater New section

Why: the new section's boards and features need placing.

A table of section B: section and resume boards at 4.100 and 4.300, the neutral section at 4.190–4.210, whistle boards at 4.500 and 4.900, Millers' Crossing at 4.700, the gradient post at 5.600. Nothing on it is a signal; a train runs it under its Line Warrant from home signal to home signal.

§ 6.5 Coldwater New section

Why: the new terminus.

Coldwater, at the head of the valley by the quarries, is Ashgrove's mirror: Fig. 6.3 and a table from the advance board at 5.750 and CW 1D at 5.780 through CW 1 (5.980), the Limit of Shunt (6.000), CW 5 (6.050), switch CW A (6.060), CW 2 and CW 4 (6.095), the stop board (6.265), CW 3 (6.285), switch CW B (6.320), CW 6 (6.330) to the buffer stop at 6.400. Track 2 has no platform.

§ 7.2 The routes at the three stations Table changed

Why: the route list now covers Wending's ten signals and Coldwater, and starters clear with the warrant in hand.

"The routes at Ashgrove and Wending": five routes each, the Wending ones using WD 3, WD 6 and WD 4 (ground) and WD 5. A closing paragraph "The section between the stations" described the offer-and-accept exchange by telephone.

Ashgrove's five routes, with "1 → Main … with warrant A in hand". Wending: Home S → 1 and → 2 (WD 1), Home N → 2 and → 1 (WD 8), 1 → S (WD 2), 2 → S (WD 4), 1 → N (WD 7), 2 → N (WD 9), each starter "with warrant A/B in hand"; the run-round as 1 → North stub (WD 7 subsidiary), North stub → 2 → South stub (WD 6 and WD 4 subsidiary), South stub → 1 (WD 5, for coupling). Coldwater's five routes with CW 1, CW 2, CW 3, CW 6 and CW 4, CW 5. The closing paragraph now refers to § 7.4.

§ 7.3 A worked example: the run-round at Wending Wording changed

Why: the run-round now uses the north stub, WD 7's and WD 4's subsidiaries, and the Line Warrant.

Step 3 "Into the headshunt" under WD 3; step 4 round the loop under WD 6 and WD 4 (ground); step 6 "Wending Box asks Ashgrove Box for the line … Ashgrove answers"; Ashgrove's mirror in one sentence.

Step 1 begins at WD 1D and ends with warrant A cancelled; step 3 "Onto the north stub" under WD 7's subsidiary, stopping short of the Limit of Shunt at 3.280; step 4 round the loop under WD 6 and WD 4's subsidiary, along platform 2; step 6 has Ashgrove issue warrant A before WD 2 clears, and the train holds it until Ashgrove cancels it. The closing sentence adds Coldwater's signals and A. Ashby's baton.

§ 7.4 Block working and the Line Warrant New section

Why: two block sections and two trains need a rule of the block.

Sections A (Ashgrove–Wending, km 0.420–2.880) and B (Wending–Coldwater, km 3.320–5.980), each with one numbered Line Warrant kept in the far box's register. The exchange in five steps: the request two minutes before time; the issue, if the section is clear and no warrant is out, with the far home cleared into the booked platform; the starter cleared to CLEAR only then; the run, holding the warrant; "train arrived complete" and the cancellation. What the driver sees of it: a starter that clears when it clears, and "warrant in hand" from the box. A "why" on choosing a warrant in the register over a token in the cab.

Rule S 28 Block working New rule

Why: the ninth principle, as a rule.

A train enters a block section only with the section's Line Warrant. The box at the far end issues the warrant when the section is clear and no other warrant for it is out; the box at the near end clears its starter only with the warrant in hand; the far box cancels the warrant when the train has arrived complete. One warrant, one train, one section.

§ 7.5 A worked example: the crossing at Wending New section

Why: the crossing is the figure the whole timetable repeats.

Trains 3 (car 1002, Ashgrove to Coldwater) and 4 (car 1003, Coldwater to Ashgrove), both away at 08:00, meeting at Wending at 08:12: two requests at 07:58 and two warrants issued; two batons at 08:00; the approaches under WD 1D and WD 8D, with the neutral section and whistle board on 1003's way down; both warrants cancelled on arrival; two more requests at 08:10; two batons at 08:12 and the trains away under WD 7 and WD 4. A note on what the second platform buys.

§ 8 One Train Working Wording changed

Why: the shuttle is now confined to Ashgrove–Wending, and the contrast table names the warrant and the distants.

Added: One Train Working "is, if you like, a single Line Warrant that is never surrendered", and "Since the Coldwater extension the shuttle is worked this way only between Ashgrove and Wending, with the Wending switches clipped for track 1 and the line beyond closed." The table's signalled column reads "One train per block section, under a Line Warrant (Rule S 28)" and "Home and starting signals with their distants; ground signals and subsidiaries for the loop."

§ 9.1 What counts as a failure Wording changed

Why: distants and warrants can fail too.

Two failures added: a distant showing CLEAR while its home shows anything but CLEAR (the home is right; the distant is reported), and a starter at CLEAR when the box has not said "warrant in hand", or a warrant the register cannot account for (the train waits until the two boxes agree).

§ 10 Glossary Glossary

Why: new words, and old ones that now reach Coldwater.

New: Crossing, Distant signal, Line Warrant, Neutral section, Resume board, Section board, Subsidiary, Through station, Whistle board. Amended: Block section (sections A and B, under a warrant), Box (three boxes and the register), Down/Up (to Coldwater), Headshunt (Wending has none), Home signal (AG 2, WD 1 and WD 8, CW 1), Main signal (governs every movement), One Train Working (Ashgrove–Wending only), Shunting movement (obeys main signals too), Starting signal (six of them, cleared with the warrant), Stub (Wending's south and north stubs), Warning distance (distants too).
