Amendment Slip S-11

Book S, tenth edition (September 2026) → eleventh edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a tenth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The eleventh edition opens Ashgrove Shed and, with it, a whole working day. Until now every car began its day at a platform and every move a Class 1 car made was a train movement; now the cars spend the night in three roads off the Ashgrove headshunt, come out under ground signals as shunts, run empty to where the first trains start, and go back to bed at dusk. That needs the shed's signals and routes in § 6 and § 7, a plain rule for what a movement is (Rule R 26 in Book R: the signal last passed decides), shunts on the register in § 10, and a description of the two box chairs of Duty 601. The masters have also changed one habit: they give line clear before the road is set when the road is not yet free, which Rule S 38 always allowed. No rule of this Book changes its number. The entries below follow the Book's order.

§ 1 Principles Paragraph added · Wording changed

Why: each edition opens with a note saying what it changed; the tenth edition's note is put in the past, as the ninth's was before it.

The chapter opened with the note "A route holds its track": "This tenth edition changes one rule and the words around it. Route locking (§ 7.6, Rule S 32) has always held a route's switches; it now holds the route's track as well … No rule changes its number."

A new note "The shed" opens the chapter: "This eleventh edition opens Ashgrove Shed: three stabling roads off the headshunt, a ladder of switches and three ground signals at the road exits, so that the Railway's day can begin in a shed and end in one instead of at a platform. With it comes the word for what a movement is … the signal last passed decides, and § 7.8 says so, with Rule R 26 behind it in Book R. The masters now give line clear before the road is set when the road is not yet free, and set the home as soon as it is, which Rule S 38 always allowed. Duty 601 puts a signaller in Ashgrove Box or Wending Box for a whole day. No rule changes its number." The tenth's note now reads "The tenth edition changed one rule … Route locking had always held a route's switches; from that edition it holds the route's track as well … No rule changed its number."

§ 6 Station layouts · 6.2 Ashgrove Sentences added · Table rows added

Why: the headshunt now carries the shed switch, and AG 5 has a second job.

The chapter's lead ended "Here is every signal, board and switch, named and placed." § 6.2 opened "Ashgrove is at the foot of the valley … Down is to the right in the diagram." The Ashgrove table listed the buffer stop, then AG 5 "Shunt from the headshunt into the station."

The lead adds "Since the eleventh edition Ashgrove also has a shed, off the headshunt, where the cars spend the night (§ 6.8)." § 6.2 adds "Halfway along the headshunt, at km 0.040, the shed switch AG S leads off to Ashgrove Shed; the shed and its three signals are drawn and listed in § 6.8, and the drawing below shows the headshunt as the run-round sees it, with AG S lying normal." The table gains a row for Switch AG S at 0.040, "toe faces Down … Normal = the headshunt on to the buffer stop, reverse = the shed lead", and AG 5's entry ends "also the second signal of every move out of the shed."

§ 6.8 Ashgrove Shed Section added · Figure added · Table added

Why: the shed is new, and every signal, switch and board on the Railway is drawn and listed in § 6.

§ 6 ended with 6.7 Fernhollow.

A new § 6.8 describes the shed in words (the shed switch AG S at km 0.040, normal for the headshunt and reverse for the shed lead; the ladder AG T and AG U; three roads of seventy metres each with buffer stops, the building over their far forty metres; shed kilometres counted from AG S into the shed, out of the shed being Down; 15 km/h throughout), draws it as Fig. 6.8 in the style of the station drawings, and tables AG S, AG T, AG U, the exit signals AG 7, AG 9 and AG 11 (facing out, which is Down, hence odd) and the buffer stops with their shed kilometres. A paragraph explains why there is no signal facing into the shed (AG 4 governs the move in, as it governs the move into the headshunt), that a car already in a road is no bar to a move into it, and that a road is a queue, the car nearest the door leaving first; a "why" box explains the facing shed switch and its price for the run-round.

§ 7.2 The routes at the four stations Table rows amended · Table rows added

Why: the run-round routes now hold the shed switch, and the shed has six routes of its own.

Ashgrove had five routes; "1 → Headshunt" was "AG B normal", and "Headshunt → 2 → Stub" was "AG B reverse, AG A reverse".

"1 → Headshunt" is "AG B normal, AG S normal … past the shed switch"; "Headshunt → 2 → Stub" is "AG S normal, AG B reverse, AG A reverse". Six rows are added: "1 → Shed 1", "1 → Shed 2" and "1 → Shed 3", under AG 4, with AG S reverse and the ladder set for the road, "the road may be occupied: stop short of what stands there"; and "Shed 1 → 1", "Shed 2 → 1" and "Shed 3 → 1", under the road's exit signal and AG 5, "for coupling: cleared into occupied track 1".

§ 7.8 The shed: shunts, and what a movement is Section added

Why: the same car is now two kinds of movement in one day, and the Railway needs the rule for which it is at any moment.

§ 7 ended with 7.7, the split and the join at Wending. The only statement of the movement class was the note in § 3.2 ("if your last signal was a main signal's amber or green, you are a train; if it was a ground signal or a subsidiary, you are shunting").

A new § 7.8 states the rule in full and refers it to Rule R 26: a main aspect makes a train (stop boards, line and station speeds, ground signals not speaking, a warrant for the section); a ground signal at SHUNT or a lit subsidiary makes a shunting move (15 km/h prepared to stop short, ground signals to obey, no stop boards, no warrant, no baton); a vehicle in a shed is a shunting move until it leaves under a main aspect. It describes a booked shunt (E-numbered, timed, its route set a minute before, no line clear and no baton, done when the car stands where it was sent) and an empty train (E-numbered, doors closed, and in every other respect a train under § 7.4). Two worked steps follow, 1003 out of road 2 at dawn and into it at dusk, signal by signal. A note records that the masters now give line clear before the road is set when the road is not free, with the words they use, and why.

§ 10.2 The block: line clear Sentences added

Why: the far box has a third answer, and the signaller may give it too.

The far box either issued the warrant and set its home, or refused in words; the paragraph ended "Ask again when it has."

"There is a third answer: when the section is free but the road into the far platform is not yet, the far box gives line clear all the same and says so, 'Line clear for 1001 to Wending. Warrant C issued; WD 10 will be cleared into track 1 when the road is free', and sets its home as soon as it can. You may do the same from your chair: give line clear on the section, and pull the home when the frame lets you (§ 7.8)."

§ 10.3 The lever frame: setting the road Paragraphs added

Why: Ashgrove Box is now a chair, and its frame has the shed's levers; shunts are worked from the frame alone.

The section described the Wending frame only, and went from the frame table straight to "Set the home before the train."

Two paragraphs before "Set the home before the train": "The Ashgrove frame", listing its levers signal by signal (AG 4 with 1 → headshunt and 1 → shed 1, 2 and 3; AG 7, AG 9 and AG 11 with shed n → 1, which clears AG 5 with it; the shed levers live into an occupied road and onto an occupied platform, dead while another route holds the headshunt or AG B); and "Shunts": a shunt is a line of the register, the working says what to pull and when ("Set AG 7 and AG 5 for 1002, shed 1 → 1 at 05:35"), there is no line clear and no baton, pull the lever a minute before the time, the register marks the shunt done when the car stands where it was sent, Replace takes back a shunt not to be made.

§ 10.7 The register and the working Words added

Why: the working has a new kind of line.

The examples of the working's advice ended with "Show the baton at 08:12 when the doors are closed".

… "and at Ashgrove 'Set AG 7 and AG 5 for 1002, shed 1 → 1 at 05:35'".

§ 10.10 Duty 601: the day from the box Section added

Why: the first whole-day duty offers two box chairs, and the signaller should know what each asks.

§ 10 ended with 10.9, Duties 501 and 502.

A new § 10.10 describes Duty 601's two chairs. Ashgrove Box: the two dawn shunts and the empty train to Coldwater, one train an hour each way thereafter, and the two dusk shunts into roads 2 and 1; nine arrivals, ten departures, four shunts. Wending Box: the hourly crossing at twelve minutes past with both neighbours asking at two minutes to; the branch car's four calls an hour into platform 1 and away under WD 7, kept clear of the crossing; line clear on section C given while the junction is busy and WD 10 pulled when the road is free; the divide at 06:07, the second lever waiting on the lock; the join at 17:13 under Call on to 2; forty-eight arrivals and forty-eight departures. A note commends the register's clock button for the quiet twenty minutes between crossings.

§ 11 Glossary Entries added · Entries amended

Why: the Book has new words, and one old word has a rule behind it now.

No entries for the shed, a shed road, a shunt, an empty train or the movement class. "Shunting movement: A movement within station limits under ground signals or a subsidiary, at up to 15 km/h, prepared to stop short of anything. It obeys main signals too." "Train movement: A movement made under main signals, from station to station."

Added: Empty train; Shed; Shed road; Shunt; Train or shunting move ("Which of the two a movement is, the signal it last passed decides … Rule R 26, § 7.8"). Shunting movement adds "stop boards do not apply to it" and refers to Train or shunting move; Train movement adds "stop boards apply to it and ground signals do not."
