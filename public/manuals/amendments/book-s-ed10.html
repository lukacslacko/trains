Amendment Slip S-10

Book S, ninth edition (September 2026) → tenth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a ninth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The tenth edition changes one rule. In the first signaller's duties it was found that the frame at Wending would let Home S → 1 and Home N → 1 be set together: two trains into one platform from opposite ends, each driver holding a CAUTION that promised clear track. The frame checked the platform only at the moment of clearing. Route locking (Rule S 32) now holds a route's track as well as its switches, and no second route can be set over track a live route holds; the call-on remains the one movement admitted to an occupied platform, and only onto one that no route is still set into. No rule changes its number. The entries below follow the Book's order.

§ 1 Principles Paragraph added · Wording changed

Why: each edition opens with a note saying what it changed; the ninth edition's note is put in the past, as the eighth's was before it.

The chapter opens with the note "Working a box": "This ninth edition adds a chapter for the person in the box. Until now every page of this Book was written for the driver … Nothing said to the driver in the earlier chapters has changed; § 10 is the same rules read from the other end of the telephone."

A new note "A route holds its track" opens the chapter: "This tenth edition changes one rule and the words around it. Route locking (§ 7.6, Rule S 32) has always held a route's switches; it now holds the route's track as well, and the frame will not set a second route over track a live route holds. Two trains are never signalled into one platform: a main aspect is cleared only into clear track, and a call-on only onto a track no route holds. The first signaller's duties showed why: a frame that checks the platform only at the moment of clearing lets two homes be set into one platform from opposite ends, and the driver, who reads the platform from the aspect, has been promised clear track by both. No rule changes its number." The ninth's note now reads "The ninth edition added a chapter … § 10 was written for the signaller … Nothing said to the driver in the earlier chapters changed …"

§ 7.6 Route locking Rule S 32 amended · Paragraphs added · Wording changed

Why: a signaller could set two homes into one platform; the frame now holds track, and the Book says what it holds, why, and why the call-on is still allowed.

The opening paragraph said a route holds its switches, "and it is the reason the junction at Wending can be worked from one box by one person without ever moving a switch under a train." Rule S 32: "A route holds its switches from the clearing of its signal until the movement has passed clear of them; no switch is moved under a route. Another route may share a switch only in the same position." The paragraph after the rule gave Home S → 1 and 1 → North stub as routes that may stand together, and went straight on to the junction.

The opening paragraph adds: "It holds its track too: every stretch the route runs over, and the platform or stub it ends on, belongs to that route while it is live, and no second route can be set over any of it," and ends "… without ever moving a switch under a train, and without ever sending two trains towards one platform." Rule S 32 now reads: "A route holds its switches and its track from the clearing of its signal until the movement has passed clear of them; no switch is moved under a route, and no second route is set over track a live route holds. Two trains are never signalled into one platform: a main aspect is cleared only into clear track, and a call-on only onto a track no route holds. The driver reads the platform from the aspect: a main aspect promises clear track to the next signal or stop board; the subsidiary promises nothing but a platform with vehicles on it." The paragraph after the rule adds Home S → 1 and Home N → 1, which share no switch and are kept apart by the track ("WD 1 is already set over track 1"). A new paragraph before the "why" box explains that the track is held for the same reason the switches are, and that the call-on is safe because it is given under the subsidiary, so the driver comes in at shunting speed prepared to stop short, and is allowed only onto a platform that no route is still set into.

§ 10.3 The lever frame: setting the road Wording changed · Sentence added · "Why" box added

Why: the frame has a fifth reason for a dead lever, and the signaller should know why the call-on lever waits.

The reasons a lever is dead: "switch WD B is held by another route", "switch WD B is fouled", "track 2 is occupied", "no warrant for section B"; "nothing you can do at the frame will clear a signal over a switch set the wrong way, into an occupied platform, or into a section without its warrant." The call-on paragraph ended: "The working says 'as a call-on behind 1002' against such a train, so that you do not reach for the wrong lever."

A fifth reason, "WD 1 is already set over track 1", and "… into an occupied platform, over track another route holds, or into a section without its warrant." The call-on paragraph adds: "The frame lets a call-on into an occupied platform, but not into one that another route is still set into: Call on to 2 stays dead while the home that admitted the first car is live, and comes alive once that car has arrived, stands, and its route has released (§ 7.6)." A "why" box follows: the frame will not let you signal two trains into one platform, whichever levers you reach for; the only way to bring a second car onto an occupied platform is the call-on, and only after the first car has arrived and its route has let go; that is the difference between a join and a collision, written as Rule S 32.

§ 11 Glossary Entry amended

Why: the definition follows the rule.

Route locking: "The holding of a route's switches from the clearing of its signal until the movement has passed clear of them. No switch is moved under a route; another route may share a switch only in the same position. Rule S 32."

Route locking: "The holding of a route's switches and its track from the clearing of its signal until the movement has passed clear of them. No switch is moved under a route; another route may share a switch only in the same position, and none is set over track a live route holds, so that two trains are never signalled into one platform. Rule S 32."
