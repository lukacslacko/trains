§ 4.7 Distant signals New section

Every home now has a distant; the driver must know what its two aspects ask.

Every home signal on the line now has a distant signal one warning distance before it: two lamps on a slate post, green above amber, with an ivory chevron plate behind the head, named after the home it repeats with the letter D. WD 1D stands at 2.68 for WD 1; AG 2D at 0.67 for AG 2; WD 8D at 3.52 for WD 8; CW 1D at 5.78 for CW 1. A distant has two aspects and neither of them is Stop. Caution (amber): the home ahead may be at Stop; reduce speed so that you can stop at it. Clear (green): the home is clear and so are the signals through the station.

Rule S 24 Distant signals (Book S, cited): At a distant at Caution, reduce speed so as to be able to stop at the home signal it repeats. A distant at Clear promises a clear run through the station.

The method is the one you already have for the advance speed board, because the two stand together at the same warning distance and ask the same of you. At a distant at Caution make the step-2 application you would make at the triangle, and be able to stop at the home. Then read the home. If it shows Stop, stop at it and wait. If it shows Caution, which is what a home into a platform shows when it is cleared for you, continue at station speed and stop at the board or the starter. On Duties 201 and 301 every home you meet shows Caution when cleared, because you stop at every station; the distant will therefore be at Caution on the approach, and that is right.

Why we do this: A home signal stands where the station begins, which is too late to learn that it is at Stop if you arrive at line speed. The distant moves the news back by a braking distance, so that a driver who cannot see the home, in fog or snow or round the shoulder of the valley, still knows what to do. It is not a signal to stop at; it is a signal to slow at, and to believe.

Note: The Ahead indicator names a distant with its home, as WD 1D (distant for WD 1) · CAUTION. In the cab there is no such courtesy: the distant is what you have until the home comes into view.

§ 4.8 Neutral sections New section · Rule D 24

Section B carries a neutral section at post 4.2; the way to work it is a rule.

Between Wending and Coldwater the overhead line is fed from both ends, and the two feeds meet at a neutral section at post 4.2: twenty metres of wire, from 4.19 to 4.21, that carry no current. A pantograph that crosses it with the motors taking current arcs at the gap, and the arc trips the substation. The section board, a black square with a white bar across the line, stands ninety metres before the gap in each direction, at 4.10 running Down and 4.30 running Up; the resume board, its bar along the line, ninety metres beyond it, at 4.30 running Down and 4.10 running Up.

Rule D 24 Neutral sections. Shut off power at the section board and coast; take power again only after the resume board. Entering the neutral section with power applied is an incident.

Steps: choose your speed before the section board (running Down you are on the 8‰ climb, so be at line speed when you shut off; running Up speed looks after itself); at the section board, controller to 0, reverser and brake alone; watch the line lamp go dark and light again; at the resume board, with the lamp lit, notch up.

Why we do this: Ninety metres at line speed is six seconds, enough for a driver to act and a train to stop drawing current; the same distance beyond puts the whole pantograph on the live wire before power is taken. The lamp in the cab is the proof of both.

§ 4.9 Whistle boards and crossings New section

Millers' Crossing at post 4.7 is the first road across the rails; Rule R 18 now asks for one long blast at the whistle board.

Millers' Crossing, a farm crossing at post 4.7, has no gates and no lamps; it has you. A whistle board, a black W on an ivory disc, stands two hundred metres before it in each direction, at 4.50 running Down and 4.90 running Up, and Rule R 18 now asks for one long blast there, for the crossing beyond it. The long blast is the same key as the short one, held. Sound it as you pass the board; a blast sounded ten seconds before the board still counts, one sounded after the crossing does not.

Why we do this: Anyone on the crossing hears the train before they can see it, because the board is placed where the line is out of sight of the road behind the shoulder of the hill. The blast is for them, not for you.

Note: Running Down, the board comes forty metres after the resume board of the neutral section: power on, then the horn, in that order, both before post 4.5 is behind you.

§ 4.10 Through stations and the block New section

Wending is a through station with two platforms, and the single line is worked by Line Warrants; the driver reads the block in the Box's messages.

Wending has become a through station with two platform tracks: platform 1 on track 1, where Down trains stop at the board at 3.165, and platform 2 on track 2, where Up trains stop at the board at 3.015. Which track you are given is the Box's decision; the home is cleared into it and the switch indicator at WD A or WD B tells you as you arrive. Every home into a platform shows Caution; every starter out of one, WD 7 (Down from track 1) or WD 4 (Up from track 2), stands at Stop until the Box has what it needs to clear it.

What it needs is the Line Warrant. The single line between two boxes is a block section: A from Ashgrove to Wending, B from Wending to Coldwater. Each section has one warrant, and a train enters the section only with it: the far box issues it when the section is clear and no other warrant for it is out; your box clears its starter only with the warrant in hand; the far box cancels it when you have arrived complete. One warrant, one train, one section: Rule S 28.

You will see the block worked in the messages: Line clear for 1002 to Coldwater. Warrant B issued; CW 1 cleared into track 1; Warrant B in hand. WD 7 cleared for the 08:12 departure; on arrival, Train 1002 arrived complete at Coldwater; warrant B cancelled. If you read Waiting for line clear, the section is not yours yet and the starter will stay at Stop past your booked time; that is not lateness of your making and it is not a reason to move. Depart on the starter and the baton, and on nothing else.

Why we do this: A signal at Stop protects the length of track you can see. The warrant protects the length you cannot: three kilometres of single line with a colleague somewhere on it, coming the other way.

Mind the crossing. When two trains are booked to pass at Wending they arrive from opposite ends into opposite platforms and leave, at the same minute, in opposite directions. Your colleague will be standing across the loop from you while you both wait for the batons. Neither of you departs until both starters are cleared, and Mr Pell will show the baton to each of you by name.

§ 12 Glossary Glossary

Eight terms added; two amended for the longer line and the kilometre plate.

Down, Up: Down is the direction of increasing kilometrage, Ashgrove towards Wending. Up is the opposite.

Kilometre post: The larger post at every full kilometre, a green numeral under the word KM. Kilometrage counts from the Ashgrove buffer stop, km 0.0, to the Wending buffer stop, km 3.3.

Warning distance: The distance from an advance speed board to the speed board it announces: …

Distant signal
A two-lamp signal with an ivory chevron plate, one warning distance before a home signal and named after it with the letter D, as WD 1D. Caution: the home may be at Stop, slow so as to stop at it. Clear: a clear run through the station. Never at Stop. § 4.7, Rule S 24.
Down, Up
Down is the direction of increasing kilometrage, Ashgrove towards Wending and Coldwater. Up is the opposite.
Kilometre post
The larger post at every full kilometre, the hectometre plate on a larger scale with a green rim, reading the kilometre with its zero, as 2.0. Kilometrage counts from the Ashgrove buffer stop, km 0.0, to the Coldwater buffer stop, km 6.4.
Line Warrant
The single authority for a train to enter a block section. Issued by the box at the far end when the section is clear and no other warrant for it is out; the starter is cleared only with it in hand; cancelled when the train has arrived complete. One warrant, one train, one section. § 4.10, Rule S 28.
Neutral section
A short length of overhead line between two feeds that carries no current, at post 4.2, from 4.19 to 4.21. Power off at the section board, on again after the resume board. § 4.8, Rule D 24.
Resume board
A black square with a white bar along the line, ninety metres beyond the neutral section: power may be taken again. § 4.8.
Section board
A black square with a white bar across the line, ninety metres before the neutral section: shut off power. § 4.8.
Subsidiary
Two small white lamps beside the head of some main signals, WD 4 and WD 7. Lit diagonally with the main at Stop they show Shunt: a shunting move may pass at shunting speed as far as the route goes; a train may not. Book S, Rule S 10.
Through station
A station the line runs through rather than ends at, with a platform track for each direction: Wending, platform 1 on track 1 for Down trains and platform 2 on track 2 for Up trains. § 4.10.
Whistle board
A black W on an ivory disc, two hundred metres before a crossing: sound one long blast. At 4.50 and 4.90 for Millers' Crossing. § 4.9, Rule R 18.
Warning distance
The distance from an advance speed board, or a distant signal, to the speed board or home it announces: (the rest of the entry as before).
