§ 5.5 Portions New section

A divided train is two trains, and the Book has to say what that means for the four conditions of departure.

"Some trains are booked to divide at Wending, and some to be joined there. A train of two Class 1 cars comes up from Ashgrove as one train under one number; at Wending it is uncoupled into two portions, one for Coldwater and one for Fernhollow, and from the moment they stand on their own each portion is a train in its own right: it has its own train number, its own booked departure, its own baton, and its own Line Warrant for the section it enters. Coming back, the two cars arrive at Wending one after the other into the same platform, the second called on behind the first, are coupled, and leave for Ashgrove as one train again under one number."

"Portions leave a station in the order they stand: the one nearest the exit goes first, and the other waits for it. Which portion that is depends on which end of the train each car has come to stand at, and the working timetable is written with that in mind; a driver reads the order from the timetable and the Box, not from a habit. Everything the driver does at the coupling, and the whole of the joining move, is in Rule D 28; the numbering and the order are in Book T."

Why we do this. "A divided train is two trains, and we say so plainly because every rule in this Book is written for one train at a time: one baton, one warrant, one departure. The moment the coupler parts, the four conditions of Rule R 20 must be met twice over, once for each portion, and neither driver may borrow the other's." The contents page lists § 5.5.

§ 9.5 Crossings Wording added

Two divided trains crossing is a crossing with four ends.

Before the "Why we do this" box: "Where two divided trains cross, as trains 7 and 8 do at Wending between 10:07 and 10:18 and trains 9 and 10 between 10:47 and 11:00, the crossing has four ends instead of two: one pair stands in platform 1 being divided while the other is being joined in platform 2, and the four departures and two arrivals are threaded through the one junction switch in the order the timetable gives. The Box holds each starter until the switch is free of the movement before it, and a portion that finds its starter at STOP at the booked time waits, as at any crossing: the delay belongs to the movement ahead."

§ 10.2 The entries Note added

The branch brings no new kind of entry, but one misreading needed naming.

After the paragraph on colleagues' entries, a note: "No new kind of entry comes with the branch. A call-on is a way of passing a home signal, not a way round it: a train that passes a home on a subsidiary that was not lit for it, or that passes any signal at STOP on the strength of a coupling to be made beyond it, has passed a signal at danger and is written up as such (S 10, S 30)."
