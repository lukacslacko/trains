Slip Amendment Slip R-4

Book R, third edition (September 2026) → fourth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a third-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The fourth edition makes one small change of substance: the kilometre post no longer carries the word KM and a green numeral. It is the same plate as the hectometre post on a larger scale, reading the kilometre with its zero, and a green rim is all that sets it apart. A driver reading posts at speed sees one shape and one style of figure the whole way along the line. Nothing was renumbered.

§ 2 Terms and directions Glossary

The definition of "Kilometre post" described the old plate.

Kilometre post. The larger post at each full kilometre: an ivory plate with a green border and a green numeral under the small word KM, reading 2 for km 2.0. It takes the place of the hectometre post at that point.

Kilometre post. The larger post at each full kilometre: the same ivory plate as the hectometre post on a larger scale, reading the kilometre with its zero in the same black figures, 2.0 for km 2.0, and distinguished only by a green rim. It takes the place of the hectometre post at that point.

Fig. 2.1 The line Figure changed

The three kilometre plates in the figure showed the old design.

Three small plates on the line at km 1, 2 and 3, each with a green rim and a green numeral: 1, 2, 3. Caption: "… the plates are the kilometre posts."

Three slightly wider plates on the line at km 1, 2 and 3, each with a green rim and black figures: 1.0, 2.0, 3.0. Caption: "… the green-rimmed plates are the kilometre posts, reading 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0."

§ 2.1 Posts and positions Wording changed

The closing sentence of the first paragraph described how a kilometre post reads.

At every full kilometre a larger kilometre post stands instead, reading simply 2.

At every full kilometre a larger kilometre post stands instead, the same plate on a larger scale with a green rim, reading the kilometre with its zero: 2.0.
