Amendment Slip S-7

Book S, sixth edition (September 2026) → seventh edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a sixth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The seventh edition is the Fernhollow branch, and with it the Railway's first junction. At the north end of Wending the line now divides at the junction switch WD J: straight on to Coldwater, or over the branch to Fernhollow, a fourth station with one platform track and its own box. Wending gains a third home signal, WD 10, on the branch; WD 8 moves outward beyond the junction; both homes carry a subsidiary that calls a train on to an occupied platform. The Book gains a chapter on route locking and a worked example of a train split and joined at Wending. Rules S 30 (Call-on) and S 32 (Route locking) are new; no rule is renumbered. The entries below follow the Book's order.

§ 1 Principles Wording changed

Why: a preface names the edition and says what a junction is; a tenth principle states the junction in one line; § 1.2 gains Fernhollow Box and M. Thorne, and the kilometrage note gains the branch.

Nine principles. Boxes: Ashgrove, Wending, Coldwater; station masters Marrow, Pell, Ashby. "Wending lies between, km 2.850 to 3.400 within its limits."

A note "The Fernhollow branch" opens the chapter: "A junction is a switch that chooses between lines." After the nine: "The Fernhollow branch added a tenth: one movement over a junction at a time. A junction is a switch that chooses between lines, and every movement over it is one route, which holds the switch from the clearing of its signal until the movement has passed clear (§ 7.6)." § 1.2 lists Fernhollow Box and M. Thorne; messages may be signed "Thorne, Fernhollow"; Wending Box exchanges line clear with three neighbours. Wending's limits run to km 3.500. "The Fernhollow branch has a kilometrage of its own, counted from the junction switch WD J (main km 3.360) to the Fernhollow buffer stop at branch km 2.320, and written with the letter F: F 0.8. On the branch, Down is away from Wending."

§ 2.3 Kinds of main signal Wording changed

Why: Wending has a third home and Fernhollow has a home and a starter; WD 7 and WD 9 now read over the junction.

Home signals: AG 2; WD 1 and WD 8; CW 1. Starters: AG 1; CW 2; WD 2, WD 4, WD 7 and WD 9 "towards Coldwater".

Home signals: AG 2; WD 1, WD 8 and WD 10; CW 1; FH 1. "WD 8 and WD 10 carry a subsidiary that can call a train on to an occupied platform (§ 2.4)." Starters: AG 1; CW 2; FH 2; WD 7 and WD 9 "northwards", and "A northward starter at Wending reads over the junction WD J, so it is the starter for Coldwater and for Fernhollow alike; the switch indicator at WD J and the box's message say which (§ 6.3)."

§ 2.4 Subsidiaries and the call-on New rule

Why: the second portion of a split train must enter a platform that already holds the first, and it is a train, not a shunting movement. The home signal itself calls it on.

§ 2.4 Subsidiaries: "Two of Wending's starting signals, WD 4 on track 2 and WD 7 on track 1, carry a subsidiary." A lit subsidiary is still a main signal at STOP.

Retitled "Subsidiaries and the call-on". Four main signals carry a subsidiary: WD 4, WD 7, WD 8 and WD 10. New sub-section "The call-on at WD 8 and WD 10": a home signal's subsidiary lit for a train means the platform ahead is occupied; the train enters at shunting speed prepared to stop short and couples only as instructed. Rule S 30 Call-on (new): "A home signal's subsidiary lit for a train is a call-on: the platform ahead is occupied. Pass at shunting speed prepared to stop short of the vehicles standing there, and couple to them only as the box has instructed. Only WD 8 and WD 10 are marked for call-on; at every other subsidiary a train is at STOP." The box announces the call-on before the driver sees it; an unannounced subsidiary at a home is treated as a failure (§ 9). Cross-cites Rule D 10.

§ 2.5 Distant signals Figures changed

Why: WD 8 and its distant moved outward past the junction; the branch has two new distants.

"WD 8D at 3.520 stands 200 m before WD 8".

"WD 8D at 3.640 stands 200 m before WD 8 … WD 10D at F 0.330 stands 250 m before WD 10, because the branch falls at 10‰ to it; FH 1D at F 1.830 stands 200 m before FH 1 on the climb." The crossing example in § 7.5 reads WD 8D at 3.640 likewise.

§ 6.1 Numbering Wording changed

Why: a fourth station code, a third home at Wending, the junction switch and the branch kilometrage.

Station codes AG, WD, CW. Wending's main signals 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 9. Distants AG 2D, WD 1D, WD 8D, CW 1D. Switches lettered A, B.

Station codes AG, WD, CW, FH. Wending's main signals 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 and 10; Fernhollow has only FH 1 and FH 2. Distants gain WD 10D and FH 1D. "The junction switch at Wending is WD J: normal for the main line to Coldwater, reverse for the Fernhollow branch." New item: the branch has its own kilometrage, written with the letter F; the letter belongs to the place, not to the signal.

§ 6.3 Wending Layout changed

Why: the north end is rebuilt as a junction. Everything south of WD B is as it was.

North end: WD 6 at 3.230, Limit of Shunt 3.280, WD 8 at 3.320, speed boards and gradient post at 3.400, WD 8D at 3.520, advance board 3.600. Fig. 6.2 showed the single line leaving north to Coldwater.

The lead paragraph names Wending a junction. Fig. 6.2 is redrawn: the north stub runs from WD B to the junction switch WD J, two lines leave it, WD 8 stands on the main line and WD 10 on the branch, each home converging on WD J, and both carry the call-on subsidiary. New and moved rows in the table: Switch WD J at 3.360 (normal = Coldwater, reverse = branch, indicator at the toe); WD 8 at 3.440; speed boards at 3.500; WD 8D at 3.640; advance board at 3.700; on the branch, WD 10 at F 0.080, speed boards 25 Up · 40 Down at F 0.140, gradient post F 0.300, WD 10D at F 0.330 (250 m, the 10‰ fall), advance board F 0.390. New note "The junction": WD 7 and WD 9 are the starters for both lines; the box's message and the indicator at WD J say which; a driver who finds them disagreeing stops short of the junction. Only one of the two homes is ever cleared at a time.

§ 6.6 Section C: the Fernhollow branch New section

Why: the branch is a line of its own, with its own kilometrage, speed and block section.

The branch leaves WD J at main km 3.360 and climbs the side valley at 10‰; kilometrage lettered F from WD J; line speed 40 km/h; block section C from WD 10 at F 0.080 to FH 1 at F 2.030, worked by Wending Box and Fernhollow Box under Line Warrant C. A table lists its boards and posts: speed boards F 0.140 and F 2.000, gradient posts F 0.300 and F 1.900, WD 10D and its advance board at F 0.330 and F 0.390, the Down advance board at F 1.800 and FH 1D at F 1.830.

§ 6.7 Fernhollow New section

Why: a fourth station.

Fernhollow is one platform track ending at a buffer stop, with no loop and no switch; a car changes ends. New Fig. 6.4 and a table: FH 1D at F 1.830; speed boards 25 Down · 40 Up at F 2.000; FH 1 home at F 2.030; FH 2 starter at F 2.085; platform F 2.100 to 2.260; stop board FERNHOLLOW at F 2.255; buffer stop at F 2.320. Fernhollow Box has two levers and a register and is worked by M. Thorne.

§ 7.1 What a route is Wording changed

Why: the call-on is a second exception to "never into occupied track", and route locking is introduced where routes are.

Note "The one deliberate exception": a shunt route for coupling, for a ground signal only.

Note "Two deliberate exceptions": the coupling shunt routes, for a ground signal or a subsidiary; and the call-on routes at WD 8 and WD 10, which admit a train to an occupied platform (§ 2.4, Rule S 30). A new paragraph says a cleared route is also locked and points to § 7.6.

§ 7.2 The routes at the four stations Table changed

Why: every route over the north end now names WD J; the branch and Fernhollow add routes of their own.

"The routes at the three stations". Wending: Home N → 2 (WD B reverse), Home N → 1 (WD B normal), 1 → N (WD B normal), 2 → N (WD B reverse).

"The routes at the four stations". Wending: Home N → 2 and → 1 now hold WD J normal too; new Home N → 2 (call-on) and Home N → 1 (call-on) under WD 8's subsidiary, cleared into occupied track; new Home B → 2, Home B → 1 and their call-on variants under WD 10 with WD J reverse; 1 → N and 2 → N hold WD J normal, with warrant B; new 1 → B and 2 → B hold WD J reverse, with warrant C. The run-round routes are unchanged and never reach the junction. New Fernhollow rows: Home S → 1 (FH 1, no switch) and 1 → S (FH 2, warrant C). A closing sentence: WD 7 and WD 9 each govern two routes, one to each line.

§ 7.4 Block working Wording changed

Why: a third block section, and section B now begins at the moved WD 8.

"The Railway has two" sections: A, km 0.420 to 2.880; B, km 3.320 to 5.980.

"The Railway has three": A as before; B from km 3.440 to 5.980; section C from Wending to Fernhollow, F 0.080 to F 2.030, worked by Wending Box and Fernhollow Box. "Sections B and C both begin at the junction, and the same starter at Wending, WD 7 or WD 9, leads into either; it is cleared with the warrant for the section the switch is set for."

§ 7.6 Route locking New rule

Why: at a junction the danger is the minute after clearing, a switch moved for the next movement while the last is still on it. The frame holds the route until the train has passed clear.

New section. A route holds its switches from the clearing of its signal; it is released only when its signal has been replaced to STOP, the approach is empty, the sections of the route are empty except the one the movement was cleared into, and no vehicle stands within 12 m of any switch on the route. A train standing in its platform does not hold the home route behind it; a departure route releases once the train has cleared the last switch. Rule S 32 Route locking (new): "A route holds its switches from the clearing of its signal until the movement has passed clear of them; no switch is moved under a route. Another route may share a switch only in the same position." Worked through for 1 → N and 1 → B at the junction.

§ 7.7 A worked example: the split and the join at Wending New section

Why: the branch exists to split a train from Ashgrove into a Coldwater portion and a Fernhollow portion, and to join them again. The example shows the signals doing it.

Train 7 (1002 and 1003 in multiple) arrives platform 1 at 10:07 and is uncoupled. 1002 leaves at 10:14 under WD 7 with WD J straight, on warrant B. 1003, holding warrant C, waits at WD 7 while the route 1 → N holds WD J; once 1002 has cleared the junction the box sets 1 → B, WD J goes reverse, and WD 7 clears a second time for the branch at 10:16. In the afternoon 1002 stands at platform 2; 1003 is called on under WD 10's subsidiary (Home B → 2, call-on), runs over the junction as a shunting movement and couples to 1002 under Rule D 10; the pair leaves as one at 11:00. A note explains why the portion at the Down end must leave first. Cross-cites Book D (Rules D 10, D 12, D 14) and Book T.

§ 9.1 What counts as a failure Wording changed

Why: two new things can disagree with the box.

Two items added: a junction indicator at WD J that disagrees with the box's message (stop short of the junction); and a home signal's subsidiary lit for a train that has not been told it is called on (treat as STOP and speak to the box).

§ 10 Glossary Glossary

Why: new words, and old ones that now reach Fernhollow.

New: Branch, Call-on, Junction, Portion, Route locking. Amended: Block section (section C), Box (Fernhollow Box), Down/Up (on the branch, away from Wending), Hectometre post (F plates), Home signal (WD 10, FH 1), Starting signal (FH 2; WD 7 and WD 9 serve both lines), Subsidiary (WD 8 and WD 10, where it is a call-on), Warning distance (250 m on the branch approach to Wending).
