Amendment Slip P-5

Book P, fourth edition (September 2026) → fifth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste this slip inside the back cover of a fourth-edition copy, or draw a new copy from the Library.

The Railway now works a whole day from its timetable, and a day has a morning and an evening: the cars come out of a shed at dawn and go back into it at dusk. The Engineer has built that shed at Ashgrove, three roads off the headshunt on the loop side through a ladder of switches, each road ending at a buffer stop under the roof. This edition records the permanent way of it: the shed switch that now divides the headshunt, the ladder, the roads and their own kilometrage, the building, and the standard to which sheds are laid out so that the next one reads the same. Rule numbers P 10 to P 14 are unchanged.

§ 1.1 The Railway's standards Row added, row amended

Why: shed roads are built to a standard of their own, and the overhead line now covers them.

The overhead line was "wired throughout, headshunts included". No row for shed roads.

"Wired throughout, headshunts and shed roads included." A new row, Shed roads: level, 15 km/h, 5 m centres; jointed rail on timber sleepers inside the building, a buffer stop at the far end of every road, a switch indicator at every switch of the ladder.

§ 2 The line Table row amended

Why: the Ashgrove row must show where the shed leaves the headshunt.

Ashgrove: "Buffer stop at 0.000, headshunt, loop with switches AG B (0.080) and AG A (0.340)…"

Ashgrove: "Buffer stop at 0.000, headshunt with the shed switch AG S at 0.040 and Ashgrove Shed off it on the loop side (§ 2.2), loop with switches AG B (0.080) and AG A (0.340)…"

§ 2.2 Ashgrove Shed New section, with Fig. 2.1

Why: the shed is the Engineer's to define, and Books S, D and T quote its switches, roads and kilometres.

No such section. The headshunt was one length from the buffer stop to AG B.

The headshunt is two lengths, divided at the shed switch AG S at km 0.040, whose toe faces the station: facing for moves into the shed, trailing for moves out, so that no shed move changes ends. From AG S the shed lead curves 30 m to the ladder switch AG T, 5 m off the headshunt's line, where road 1 begins; a second lead curves on to AG U, 10 m off, where road 2 begins; a third to the start of road 3, 15 m off. Three parallel roads at 5 m centres, 70 m long each, level, 15 km/h, a buffer stop at the end of every one. Shed kilometres run from AG S into the shed (road 1 from shed 0.030, road 2 from 0.061, road 3 from 0.091; buffer stops at 0.100, 0.131 and 0.161), and a position on a road is quoted with the word, "road 2 at shed 0.110". Out of the shed is the main line's Down direction, hence the odd-numbered exit signals AG 7, AG 9 and AG 11 at the roads' mouths. The building stands over the far 40 m of road 1 and the corresponding lengths of roads 2 and 3, 100 m by 16 m, doors towards the ladder; switch indicators at AG S, AG T and AG U. Fig. 2.1 is the plan, drawn six times larger across the line than along it; a table gives each switch, road and buffer stop with its kilometre; a note says why the shed is on the loop side, why a ladder, and that sheds are laid out as a standard piece so that the next one reads the same.

§ 6 Features of the line Table row added

Why: the table of features names every place another Book rules over; the shed is one.

The table ended at Millers' Crossing.

A further row, Ashgrove Shed, shed 0.000–0.161: the shed switch AG S at km 0.040, the exit signals AG 7, AG 9 and AG 11, the buffer stops at the road ends (§ 2.2); whose rule: Book S for the shunting, Book T for the stabling.

§ 7 Looking after the line Item added

Why: the ladder is walked over twice a day by crews and takes every shed move.

The list of what wears ended with "The junction and the curve."

A further item, "The shed ladder": three switches close together take every car that goes to bed and every car that gets up, at walking pace and on the same few metres of blade; nothing on it wears fast, but crews walk over it morning and evening, and a fastening loosened by a boot is reported before a train finds it.

§ 8 Glossary Entries added

Why: three new words.

No entries for the shed.

Shed (a building with roads under its roof where vehicles stable; Ashgrove Shed, three roads off the headshunt, § 2.2), Shed road (one stabling track of a shed, from its ladder switch to its buffer stop, quoted by number and shed kilometre) and Ladder (a run of switches each leading off the last, so that several parallel roads are reached from one lead; AG T and AG U).
