Amendment Slip D-9

Book D, eighth edition (September 2026) → ninth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of an eighth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The first whole day, and the shed it starts from. Until now every duty began with a car standing at a platform as if it had been there all night, and ended with it standing at another. The weekday working of Duty 601 begins at 05:00 in Ashgrove Shed, three roads off the headshunt, and ends there twelve hours later; the cars come out as shunts under the ground signals, one runs empty to Coldwater to begin the Up service, the pair divides at Wending, and everything goes back into its road at dusk. That brought four things into this Book that were not in it: the shed and the two shunts of the day, what makes a movement a train or a shunt (Book R's new Rule R 26), empty trains, and riding in a train that a colleague is driving, with the dividing of a train that follows from it. No rule of Book D changes its number or its meaning. The timetable itself is Book T's.

§ 1.1 Where you are Paragraph added

The four places of the eighth edition did not cover a driver sitting in a cab of a train somebody else drives, which Mr Penrose's turn on Duty 601 does twice a day.

There is a fifth place, for one kind of duty: in a cab of a train that a colleague is driving from another cab. You are then riding; the desk before you is alive, but the train is not yours (§ 4.15).

§ 3 Preparing a stabled vehicle Lead sentence amended; § 3.1 and § 3.2 added

A car that starts its day in the shed is prepared in the dark and leaves as a shunt, not as a departure; the chapter said only how to prepare a car at a platform.

… You arrive on the ground beside it with fifteen minutes in hand. This is how those fifteen minutes go.

… You arrive on the ground beside it with fifteen minutes in hand, at a platform or, on a whole day's work, in the shed. This is how those fifteen minutes go.

§ 3.1 Booking on at the shed describes Ashgrove Shed (three roads off the headshunt through the shed switch AG S and the ladder AG T and AG U, seventy metres each to a buffer stop, the building over the far forty), where each car stands (road 1: 1002 at the door with 1001 coupled behind; road 2: 1003 at the door with the spare 1004 behind; A ends towards the door), and how booking on differs: the rear cab is inside the shed, and the rear car of a coupled pair prepares only itself. A note gives the movement class of a vehicle in the shed (Rule R 26).

§ 3.2 Out of the shed: the first shunt: a minute before the booked time the Box sets the road's exit signal (AG 7, AG 9 or AG 11) and AG 5 to SHUNT; no warrant and no baton; one short blast and out at 15 km/h over the ladder, AG S, the headshunt and AG B onto platform 1; stop with the rear of the car level with the board; a train from the moment AG 1 is passed at CLEAR.

§ 4.14 Shunts, empties, and what a movement is Section added

The working timetable now has three kinds of movement, and the Book had described only the passenger train and, in passing, the run-round.

A table of the three kinds (passenger train, empty train, shunt) with their numbers, authority, speed and what applies to each; empty trains described (trains in every way, doors kept closed); shunts described (never leave station limits, ground signals and subsidiaries, 15 km/h, stop short of what stands in the road); Rule R 26 cited in full; and the principle that the signal last passed decides what a movement is: a main aspect makes a train, a ground signal or subsidiary at SHUNT a shunting move, a car in the shed a shunting move until it leaves under a main aspect. The Speed indicator and the Ahead list show which you are.

§ 4.15 Riding, and dividing a train Section added

Entering a cab of a train a colleague drives no longer takes the train from them; the rider's cab refuses power, brake and reverser, and takes the car only when it is uncoupled. The divide at Wending is worked from the rider's side.

Riding described (the panel's "Riding · Farrow has the train from 1002", the refusal "1002 has the train: you are riding", what remains yours); dividing described (the front portion waits for the rear to be uncoupled; the rider uncouples and takes the free-end cab, which then answers; Tail on the new rear cabs); and Mr Penrose's day in seven steps, from booking on behind 1002 in road 1 to the call-on behind it at 17:13 and the ride home.

§ 7.1 Into the shed Section added

The chapter ended the day at a platform; a day worked to the timetable ends with a shunt into the shed and the stabling done there.

The last shunt of the day: AG 4 at SHUNT with AG S lying for the shed and the ladder for the road, a minute before the booked time; one short blast, shunting speed, through AG B and past AG 5 into the road; stop three metres short of the buffer stop or two metres short of the car already there; then the stabling steps from step 3. Notes: two cars in one road do not couple, and a pair that comes home coupled stays coupled; the buffer stops are inside a building, so be at walking pace by the door.

§ 11 Duty sheets · Duty 601 Card and three paragraphs added

Duty 601 is the first duty with more than one driving chair; each needs its own "What to expect".

A card for Duty 601, A Day on the Valley: the stock in the shed at 05:00; E1 and E3 for 1003, E5 for the pair; train 1 and the divide at Wending; the hourly main-line pattern crossing at twelve past; the half-hourly branch; E8 at 16:35; the join at Wending at 17:13; E10 at 17:40. Then what to expect driving 1002 (Ms Farrow: the pair out of road 1, the divide, even hours Down and odd hours Up, train 24 waiting on Wending platform 2 for the call-on, the pair into road 1), driving 1003 (Mr Hale: the earliest book-on, E1 and the empty run to Coldwater, odd hours Down and even hours Up, into road 2 behind the spare at 16:35) and driving 1001 (Mr Penrose: the rider's day, the branch, the call-on, the ride home).

§ 12 Glossary Entries added and amended

New words in the Book.

Shunting move. Any movement made under a ground signal within station limits, at 15 km/h or less.

Shunting move. A movement within station limits under ground signals and subsidiaries at 15 km/h or less: a run-round, a move between the shed and a platform. What a movement is, the signal it last passed decides: a ground signal or subsidiary at SHUNT makes a shunting move, a main aspect makes a train. Stop boards do not apply to a shunting move. § 4.14, Rule R 26.

Added: Divide, Empty train, Riding, Shed, Shed road.
