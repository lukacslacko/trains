Amendment Slip D-8

Book D, seventh edition (September 2026) → eighth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a seventh-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

One control, described properly at last. Rule R 18 has always asked for two blasts, a short one before moving and a long one at a whistle board, but the seventh edition described the horn as "one press, one short blast" and said nothing about how a long blast is given. Drivers who held the key down got a string of short blasts in the Messages tab and no long one anywhere. The horn now sounds for as long as it is held: released within a second and a half it is a short blast, held a second and a half or longer it is a long blast, and the Messages tab names the one you gave. The whistle board is satisfied only by a long blast. No rule changes its number or its meaning.

§ 2.1 Controls · Horn Paragraph rewritten

The old paragraph described a horn that could give only short blasts, and gave no way to sound the long one that Rule R 18 asks for at a whistle board.

One press, one short blast. Sound it before every movement from rest (Rule R 18), including shunting moves and including the second half of a change of ends. Nobody on the platform has to guess.

The horn sounds for as long as the key is held, and a blast is judged by its length: released within a second and a half it is a short blast; held a second and a half or longer it is a long blast. A tap gives the short one and a deliberate hold gives the long one, and the Messages tab records which you gave, as "One short blast." or "One long blast." The Horn button on the panel works the same way, pressed and held with the mouse. Sound one short blast before every movement from rest (Rule R 18), including shunting moves and including the second half of a change of ends. Nobody on the platform has to guess. The long blast belongs to the whistle board (§ 4.9).

§ 2.3 The keys · Horn Table row changed

The row listed the horn as a one-shot control.

Horn · press: short blast · hold: long blast · H tap, or hold for a second and a half

§ 4.1 Starting List item changed

§ 4.9 Whistle boards and crossings Sentences changed

The paragraph said the long blast was "the same key, held" without saying how long, and the crossing used to accept any blast; it now insists on a long one.

… The long blast is the same key as the short one, held. Sound it as you pass the board; a blast sounded ten seconds before the board still counts, one sounded after the crossing does not.

… The long blast is the same key as the short one, held: keep H down for a second and a half or more, and let the Messages tab confirm "One long blast." A short blast does not satisfy the board, however many you give; pass the crossing without a long one and the Incident Book records it as passed without a blast. Sound it as you pass the board; a long blast sounded ten seconds before the board still counts, one sounded after the crossing does not.

§ 12 Glossary · Whistle board Wording changed

The entry now says what a long blast is.

Whistle board. A black W on an ivory disc, two hundred metres before a crossing: sound one long blast. At 4.50 and 4.90 for Millers' Crossing. § 4.9, Rule R 18.

Whistle board. A black W on an ivory disc, two hundred metres before a crossing: sound one long blast, the horn held a second and a half or more. At 4.50 and 4.90 for Millers' Crossing. § 4.9, Rule R 18.
