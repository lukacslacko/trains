The fifth edition opens the line to Coldwater. Wending becomes a through station with two platforms, the single line is worked in two block sections under Line Warrants, distant signals stand before the home signals, a neutral section and a farm crossing lie between Wending and Coldwater, and a colleague may be working the other train. Book R takes the terms, the block as it touches the driver, the long blast at a whistle board, the crossing at Wending and three Incident Book entries; the signalling itself is in Book S, the driving in Book D, the timetable in the new Book T. No rule was renumbered.

Neutral section (a gap in the overhead line where two feeds meet, km 4.19–4.21, through which no power can be taken; section board before, resume board after; Rule D 24). Whistle board (a black W on an ivory disc before a crossing; one long blast; Rule R 18). Crossing (a road across the line on the level: Millers' Crossing at km 4.70, whistle boards 200 m before it each way; also two trains passing at a station, § 9.5). § 3.5 The block New section

The block as it touches the driver: what a Line Warrant is, who holds it, and why the starter is its whole face.

The single line between two stations is a block section, and it holds one train at a time. No train enters a section without the section's Line Warrant. The two Boxes at its ends exchange it by telephone: the Box that wants to send a train asks for line clear; the Box at the far end gives it only if the section is clear and no warrant for it is out, sets its home signal for the arriving train, and issues the warrant; the sending Box then clears its starter; when the train has arrived complete at the far end, that Box cancels the warrant and the section is free again. Section A lies between Ashgrove and Wending, section B between Wending and Coldwater. The whole of the working is in Rule S 28.

The driver's part in this is simple, and it is the point of the arrangement: the starter does not clear without the warrant. A driver who leaves on a cleared starter has the section to themselves and need not think about the block at all. A driver who finds the starter at STOP at the booked time waits; the Box will say why, and it is usually that a colleague has not yet arrived at the far end.

Why we do this. One warrant, one train, one section. We could have given each driver a token to carry, and some railways do; but the token would have to be handed over, carried back, and never be in two places at once, and every one of those is a thing that can go wrong on a platform. A warrant that lives in the Boxes and shows itself only as a cleared starter cannot be dropped, forgotten or fought over. The signal is its whole face.

Rule R 18 Horn Wording changed

The long blast at a whistle board, for Millers' Crossing, joins the rule; the explanation says why the two blasts differ.

Before moving from rest, sound one short blast. When the baton has been shown, the blast is also the driver's acknowledgement of it.

Before moving from rest, sound one short blast. When the baton has been shown, the blast is also the driver's acknowledgement of it.

At a whistle board, sound one long blast, for the crossing beyond it.

Why we do this gains: "The long blast at a whistle board is for people who cannot see the train at all: a farm cart at Millers' Crossing hears the horn before it sees the car, and the board stands far enough back that the horn arrives first. Short means 'I am moving'; long means 'I am coming'. Keep them different."

§ 9.5 Crossings New section

What a crossing in the timetable is, and that it waits for the late train.

Where two trains are booked to pass each other at a through station, the timetable shows a crossing: both arrive, both stand, and both leave, each into the section the other has just cleared. A crossing waits for the late train. The Box cannot clear a starter into a section until the train coming the other way has arrived complete and its warrant is cancelled, so if your colleague is late, your starter stays at STOP past the booked time, and the delay is theirs, not yours. Wait, and do not write it in the Book; the Box has already noted it.

Why we do this. A crossing is the one place on a single line where the timetable and the block are the same thing. We put the crossing at Wending because it has two platforms and a Box, and we book a few minutes of stand at each, so that a train a minute or two late still meets its partner without either driver having to hurry.
