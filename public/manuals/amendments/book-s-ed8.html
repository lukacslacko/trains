Amendment Slip S-8

Book S, seventh edition (September 2026) → eighth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a seventh-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The eighth edition moves the subsidiary. Until now the small black box with its two white lamps was drawn and described beside the head of the main signal; a box off to one side breaks the outline of the post and reads, from the cab, as a second signal standing next to the first. It is now fixed on the post directly under the main head, so that main and subsidiary are one signal on one structure. Fig. 2.2 is redrawn, § 2.4 and the glossary are reworded, and the preface gains a note. The indications, the four signals that carry a subsidiary (WD 4, WD 7, WD 8, WD 10) and the call-on at WD 8 and WD 10 are unchanged; no rule changes its number or its meaning. Fig. 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 and 4.1 are the reference drawings from which the other Books copy their signals and boards. The entries below follow the Book's order.

§ 1 Principles Wording changed · Paragraph added

Why: each edition opens with a note saying what it changed; the seventh edition's note is put in the past, as the sixth edition's was before it.

The chapter opens with the note "The Fernhollow branch": "This seventh edition opens the Railway's first junction. … everything in this edition that is new follows from that: …"

A new note "The subsidiary under the head" opens the chapter: "This eighth edition changes one drawing and the words around it. A subsidiary is no longer fixed beside the head of its main signal but on the post directly under it, so that main and subsidiary are one signal on one structure (§ 2.4, Fig. 2.2). No rule changes its number or its meaning. The signal drawings of § 2 and § 4 are the reference drawings from which the other Books copy." The Fernhollow note now reads "The seventh edition opened the Railway's first junction. … everything in that edition that was new followed from that: …"

§ 2.4 Subsidiaries and the call-on Wording changed

Why: the subsidiary is fixed on the post directly under the main head, not beside it, and the paragraph on why there is no separate ground signal now also says why the box is under the head.

"A subsidiary is a small black box beside the head with two white lamps, the same lamps as a ground signal. Normally they are dark." … "The subsidiary puts the shunting indication on the starter itself, where it is the only thing to read. At Wending the run-round …"

"A subsidiary is a small black box with two white lamps, the same lamps as a ground signal, fixed on the post directly under the main head: one signal on one structure, the head above and the box below. Normally the lamps are dark." … "The subsidiary puts the shunting indication on the starter itself, where it is the only thing to read. The box hangs under the head rather than beside it for the same reason: a box off to one side breaks the outline of the post and reads, from the cab, as a second signal standing next to the first. At Wending the run-round …"

§ 2.4 Fig. 2.2 Figure changed

Why: the drawing shows the subsidiary where it is now fixed, in the style and colours of Fig. 2.1 and 2.3, and is the reference drawing for the main signal with a subsidiary.

Two drawings of WD 7 at STOP with the subsidiary box drawn to the right of the head, level with the red lamp; left with the box dark, right with the two white lamps lit diagonally. Caption: "WD 7 with its subsidiary. Left, everything at STOP. Right, the main head still at STOP and the subsidiary showing SHUNT: a shunting movement may pass; a train may not."

Two drawings of WD 7 at STOP with the subsidiary box fixed on the post directly under the head, a little narrower than the head, the name plate below the box; left with the box dark, right with the two white lamps lit diagonally, rising to the right, under the red lamp. Caption: "WD 7 with its subsidiary on the post under the head. Left, everything at STOP. Right, the main head still at STOP and the subsidiary showing SHUNT: a shunting movement may pass; a train may not."

§ 10 Glossary Glossary

Why: the entry for the subsidiary says where it is fixed.

Subsidiary: "Two small white lamps beside the head of a main signal (WD 4, WD 7, WD 8, WD 10). …"

Subsidiary: "Two small white lamps in a box on the post directly under the head of a main signal (WD 4, WD 7, WD 8, WD 10). …"
