Amendment Slip D-2

Book D, first edition (September 2026) → second edition (September 2026). Issued 8 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a first-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The second edition brings the Driving Manual into line with the new speed-board arrangements: reductions are announced by an advance speed board one warning distance ahead, and a limit belongs to the whole train, not to the cab. Six passages change; nothing is renumbered.

§ 2.2 Indicators Wording changed

The Limit indicator now accounts for the whole train, so its description had to say so.

Limit — The speed limit that applies where you are now.

Limit — The lowest limit under any part of the train. It falls as the front reaches a lower board and rises only once the rear has passed a higher one.

§ 4.2 Speeds Wording changed

The table row for line speed and the paragraph beneath the table now state the whole-train rule of Rule R 10; a Why box explains it.

Line speed, 50: Between the stations, from the speed board that says 50 to the one that says 25.

The Limit indicator shows the figure in force. The limit changes at the board, not before it, so a train may not pass a 25 board at 40 on the grounds that it is slowing down. Begin braking early enough to be at the new speed when you reach the board.

Line speed, 50: Between the stations. Yours once the whole train has passed the board that says 50; over for the front of the train at the board that says 25.

A limit applies to the whole train, and a train's limit is the lowest limit under any part of it (Rule R 10). A lower limit is yours from the moment the front reaches its board: a train may not pass a 25 board at 40 on the grounds that it is slowing down. A higher limit is yours only once the rear of the train has passed its board: leaving station limits, the whole train must be past the 50 board before you notch up. The Limit indicator keeps this account for you; it falls as the front reaches a lower board and rises only when the last vehicle has cleared a higher one.

The tail of the train is still in the slow section until it has passed the board. A locomotive that opens up the moment its cab passes the 50 board drags its coach through station limits at forty. Reductions are announced ahead, so that the front can be at the new speed when it arrives; rises are not, because nothing is lost by waiting a train-length.

§ 4.2 Braking for a reduction New section

Advance speed boards now stand 200 m before each 25 board; this passage says how to use them, and what to do in fog or snow.

Braking for a reduction

A reduction is announced one warning distance ahead by an advance speed board: the same black numeral, on an ivory triangle with its point up (Book S, § 4). On the Ashgrove–Wending line the warning distance is 200 metres. The advance board for the 25 at 2.85 stands at 2.65 running Down, fifty metres beyond post 2.6; the one for the 25 at 0.45 stands at 0.65 running Up, fifty metres beyond post 0.7. Rises get no advance board: a 50 board simply stands where the 50 begins.

  1. At the advance board, step 2. On the Class 1 car, and on the Class 4 with one coach, step 2 gives about 0.5 m/s² on the level.
  2. Count the distance. At that rate a train loses the twenty-five between 50 and 25 in about 145 metres. You reach the speed board with some fifty metres in hand. That margin is the allowance for reaction, for fog and for snow; it is not an invitation to apply later.
  3. Check at the speed board. Be at 25 as the front reaches it. If you are above, step 3 at once and note it; if you are well under, ease to step 1, but do not release before the board.
  4. Hold it. Release once at 25 and keep 25: the Limit indicator shows 25 through station limits and will not show 50 again until the rear of the train has cleared the 50 board on the way out.

In fog or falling snow, brake at the advance board without waiting to see the speed board. The board will arrive when the count says it will, and the posts confirm the count: running Down, 2.7 and 2.8 pass while you brake and the board is fifty metres beyond 2.8; running Up, 0.6 and 0.5 pass and the board is fifty metres beyond 0.5.

§ 4.3 Braking to a stop board — Judging distance by the posts Wording changed · Table changed

The posts paragraph and table now place the advance boards and say when the 50 boards take effect.

The 25 km/h boards stand at 2.85 and 0.45, between posts: running Down you should be at 25 before 2.9, running Up before 0.4.

Table: 0.4 — Home signal AG 2 20 m on the Down side; speed boards 25 and 50 at 0.45.  ·  2.8 — Speed boards 25 and 50 at 2.85; home signal WD 1 at 2.88, twenty metres short of 2.9.

The 25 km/h boards stand at 2.85 and 0.45, between posts, and their advance boards two hundred metres earlier at 2.65 and 0.65. Running Down, make your application fifty metres past 2.6 and be at 25 before 2.9; running Up, make it fifty metres past 0.7 and be at 25 before 0.4. The 50 boards at the same places are yours once the rear of the train has passed them.

§ 12 Glossary Glossary

Two terms added.

Advance speed board
A black numeral on an ivory triangle, point up, announcing a lower limit one warning distance ahead. It gives the figure; the speed board beyond it gives the place.
Warning distance
The distance from an advance speed board to the speed board it announces: 200 m on the Ashgrove–Wending line, enough to lose 25 km/h at 0.5 m/s² with fifty metres to spare.
