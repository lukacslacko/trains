Amendment Slip S-5

Book S, fourth edition (September 2026) → fifth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a fourth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The fifth edition changes one sign. The kilometre post no longer carries the word KM and a green numeral: it is now the hectometre plate on a larger scale, reading the kilometre with its zero in the same black figures, and only its green rim tells it apart. Nothing else changed and no rule was renumbered.

§ 4 Boards, table Wording changed

Why: the row describing the posts now describes the new kilometre plate.

Hectometre post, Kilometre post — ivory plate on a slate post, black figures (1.4) or a green numeral under KM (2).

Hectometre post, Kilometre post — ivory plate on a slate post, black figures (1.4); at each full kilometre a larger plate with a green rim, reading the kilometre with its zero (2.0).

§ 4.3 Posts Wording changed

Why: a plate that reads like its neighbours is read at a glance; the word KM and the change of colour cost more than they gave.

At every full kilometre the plate is larger, bordered in green, and carries the kilometre alone in a green numeral under the small word KM: the post at 2 km reads 2.

At every full kilometre the plate is the same plate on a larger scale, rimmed in green, and reads the kilometre with its zero in the same black figures: the post at 2 km reads 2.0. A plate that reads like its neighbours is read at a glance; the green rim is all the distinction a full kilometre needs.

§ 4.3 Fig. 4.2 Figure changed

Why: the drawing of the kilometre post follows the new plate.

The kilometre post was drawn as a taller green-bordered plate with the small word KM above a large green numeral 2.

The kilometre post is drawn as a larger ivory plate with a green rim reading 2.0 in black, beside the hectometre post reading 1.4. The caption now reads: "A hectometre post and a kilometre post. The figures are the distance from the Ashgrove buffer stop; the kilometre plate is larger and rimmed in green, and reads like its neighbours."

§ 10 Glossary Glossary

Why: the definition of the kilometre post follows the plate.

Kilometre post — The larger, green-bordered post at each full kilometre, reading the kilometre alone under the word KM (2). A position reference, not a signal.

Kilometre post — The larger, green-rimmed plate at each full kilometre, reading the kilometre with its zero in the same black figures as the hectometre posts (2.0). A position reference, not a signal.
