Amendment Slip T-3

Book T, second edition (September 2026) → third edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a second-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The third edition follows the first whole day worked from the timetable. Until now every duty began with a car already at a platform; the weekday working begins in Ashgrove Shed before six and ends there after half past five, and the Traffic Office now writes the timetable once, as data, from which every box's register, every colleague's turns and every duty sheet are read. The Book gains a chapter, § 9, with Rule T 18 and a diagram of the dawn; the glossary becomes § 10 and keeps its place at the end. Rules T 10 to T 16 keep their numbers. The entries below follow the Book's order.

Cover Wording changed

Why: the subtitle now names what the Book has learned to do.

The working timetable of the Railway: how trains are numbered, timed, crossed, divided, joined and kept apart.

The working timetable of the Railway: how trains are numbered, timed, crossed, divided, joined and kept apart, and how a whole day is worked from shed to shed.

§ 1 Why a timetable Note added

Why: a reader picking up the third edition should know at once what it adds.

The weekday working. This third edition adds § 9: a whole weekday from Ashgrove Shed at dawn to Ashgrove Shed at dusk, written once as data and worked from by every box, every colleague and every duty sheet, with Rule T 18. The second edition's addition, the branch and the portions of § 5, stands as it was.

§ 1.3 Two timetables Wording changed

Why: the Book now prints two kinds of working.

Every driver and every box works from it. This Book prints it in § 6.

Every driver and every box works from it. This Book prints the duties' timetables in § 6 and the whole weekday working in § 9.

§ 2.2 Blocks of numbers Table changed

Why: a day of hourly trains needs more numbers than a morning; empties and shunts need numbers of their own.

1–9 passenger trains over the full line; 11–19 the shuttle; 31–39 branch trains.

1–29 passenger trains over the full line: 3 to 6 in Duty 301, 7 to 10 in Duty 401, 1 to 24 on the weekday working. 11–19 the shuttle, with the note that a block belongs to a working: on a day the shuttle runs, the weekday working does not. 31–89 branch trains; Duty 401 uses 31 to 34, the weekday working runs from 31 to 74. New row: E1–E99, empties and shunts: an empty train positioning a car, or a shunt between a shed road and a platform; odd for Down (out of Ashgrove Shed is Down), even for Up.

§ 6 The working timetable Wording changed

Why: the chapter is one of two, and the box duties share its tables.

Four duties are worked at present. Each is set out as the boxes and the drivers read it …

Four driving duties are set out here as the boxes and the drivers read them … Duties 501 and 502 work the timetables of 301 and 401 from Wending Box. The weekday working, a whole day from shed to shed, is § 9.

§ 8.1 What is on it Paragraph added

Why: a weekday sheet names shed roads and trains, and a driver may ride.

On the weekday working (§ 9) the sheet carries a train column and names places that are not platforms: AG shed 1 → AG 1 is a shunt out of the shed, and a shunt is marked at the moment it began, with no baton looked for. A driver who rides in a colleague's train has no leg for the ride; the brief says what to do before the first leg begins.

§ 9 The weekday working New chapter

Why: the first whole day worked from the timetable, and the timetable itself now the thing the Railway works from.

9.1 The timetable as data. A service (number, kind: passenger, empty or shunt; vehicle; calls), a call (station, track, which may be a platform or a shed road, arrival, departure; two calls at one station in succession are a shunt), coupled to a service until the train divides, joins a service on arrival, the crew diagram (driver, vehicle, sign on, sign off, turns) and the stabling (a road, its vehicles from the buffer stop outwards, coupled or not).

Rule T 18 The whole day. Every movement of a vehicle in a day is in the working timetable: the shunts out of the shed and into it, the empties that position a car, the passenger trains. The boxes plan for what the timetable moves and for nothing else; a driver's diagram is the timetable's turns in order; a vehicle is stabled at night where the timetable stables it.

9.2 The stock at dawn. Ashgrove Shed road 1: 1001, then 1002 at the door end, coupled; road 2: 1004, then 1003 at the door end, not coupled; road 3 empty. Cars stand with their A ends facing the door.

9.3 The shed and the positioning. E1, 1003, shed 2 → platform 1, 05:15–05:19; E3, 1003 empty, Ashgrove 05:25 → Wending 05:32/05:34 → Coldwater 05:43; E5, the pair 1002+1001, shed 1 → platform 1, 05:35–05:39. A shunt moves on ground signals and subsidiaries at shunting speed with neither warrant nor baton and stops short of what stands in the road; an empty train runs under the same signals, warrants and boards as a passenger train with its doors closed (Rule R 26).

9.4 The main line. Down trains 1 + 2(h − 6) leave Ashgrove on the hour 06:00–16:00, call Wending 1 at h:07/h:12, arrive Coldwater h:21; Up trains 2 + 2(h − 6) leave Coldwater on the hour 06:00–17:00, call Wending 2 at h:08/h:12, arrive Ashgrove h:21; 1002 works Down at even hours and Up at odd, 1003 the other way about; every crossing at Wending at twelve past. Train 1 leaves with 1001 coupled and divides at Wending (1002 on at 06:12, 1001 as 31 at 06:16); train 24, the last Up, stands on Wending platform 2 from 17:08 to 17:20 for the branch car to be called on behind it.

9.5 The branch. 1001 twice an hour, platform 1 at Wending, clear of the crossing: with n = 32 + 4(h − 6), Fernhollow h:34 → Wending h:43 (n), Wending h:46 → Fernhollow h:54 (n + 1), Fernhollow (h+1):04 → Wending (h+1):13 (n + 2), Wending (h+1):16 → Fernhollow (h+1):24 (n + 3), h = 06 to 16, the last two not at h = 16; then 74, Fernhollow 17:04 → Wending 2 17:13, called on to train 24. The (h+1):04 train is given line clear while the junction is still held for the crossing, and finds WD 10 cleared as soon as the Down train has left it.

9.6 Dusk. 22 reaches Ashgrove 16:21 and E8 puts 1003 into road 2 at 16:35, short of 1004; 74 is called on to 24 at Wending under WD 10, couples, pipe connected, brake proved; 24 leaves Wending 17:20 for Ashgrove 17:29; E10 puts the pair into road 1 at 17:40, 1002 leading in, so that 1001 stands at the door end for the morning.

9.7 The crew diagrams. Hale, 1003, on 05:05 off 16:50: E1, E3, 2, 3, 6, 7 … 22, E8. Farrow, 1002, on 05:25 off 17:55: E5, 1, 4, 5, 8 … 21, 24, E10. Penrose, 1001, on 05:25 off 17:55: 31 to 74, riding in the pair to Wending and home after the join. Corry, 1004, spare.

9.8 What the boxes see (the register, shunts as "05:35 shunt from shed 1 to platform 1", Ashgrove setting the shed road a minute before time) and 9.9 What a driver sees (the train column, places such as AG shed 1 → AG 1, no leg for a ride).

Fig. 9.1, the time–distance diagram of the dawn, 05:00 to 07:30, with the shed as a band below the Ashgrove line: the two shunts, the empty run, the divide at 06:07, the crossings at 06:12 and 07:12 and the branch's first trips.

9.10 Duty 601, A Day on the Valley. Five chairs: drive 1002, 1003 or 1001 with that driver's turns, or work Wending or Ashgrove Box; the other four are taken by colleagues. A driver's duty is done when the car is stabled in the shed at the end of the diagram; a signaller's when every train is home and the register is done.
