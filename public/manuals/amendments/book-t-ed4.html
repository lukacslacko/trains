Amendment Slip T-4

Book T, third edition (September 2026) → fourth edition (September 2026). Issued 10 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a third-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The fourth edition follows the Traffic Office's timetable view: the time–distance diagram of Fig. 7.2 and Fig. 9.1 drawn for the whole day and kept alive, with the working timetable, the crew diagrams and the stock working read from the same data, and, during Duty 601, the day as run drawn beside the day as booked. The Book gains § 9.11 and three words in the glossary; nothing is renumbered, and Rules T 10 to T 18 keep their numbers. The entries below follow the Book's order.

§ 1 Why a timetable Note changed

Why: a reader picking up the fourth edition should know at once what it adds; the third edition's note is put in the past.

The weekday working. This third edition adds § 9: a whole weekday from Ashgrove Shed at dawn to Ashgrove Shed at dusk, written once as data and worked from by every box, every colleague and every duty sheet, with Rule T 18. The second edition's addition, the branch and the portions of § 5, stands as it was.

The timetable view. This fourth edition adds § 9.11: the Traffic Office's four leaves on the weekday working, as the game shows them, the train graph, the working timetable, the crew diagrams and the stock working, and, during Duty 601, the day as run drawn beside the day as booked. The third edition added § 9, the whole weekday from Ashgrove Shed at dawn to Ashgrove Shed at dusk written once as data, with Rule T 18; it stands as it was, as does the second edition's § 5.

§ 8.1 What is on it Paragraph added

Why: the sheet is one driver's turn; the whole day is on another page.

The sheet is one turn. For the whole day at a glance, every driver's turns on one page and every train on one drawing, the Traffic Office keeps the timetable view (§ 9.11).

§ 9.11 The timetable view New section

Why: the drawings of § 7 and § 9 were drawings of a morning; the Railway now keeps the drawing of the whole day, and keeps it alive while the day is run.

Where it opens: from the home page under The Traffic Office; in Duty 601 from the Timetable button in the top bar, laid over the running duty, taken away by the button or Escape. Four leaves; a car keeps one colour on every leaf (1001 amber, 1002 green, 1003 red, 1004 slate).

The train graph. Time across, 05:00 to 19:00, hour lines and quarter-hour ticks; distance down. The main line the upper panel (Ashgrove at the top with a thin Ashgrove Shed row above it, Wending, Coldwater at the foot); the branch a panel of its own beneath, headed THE BRANCH, Wending at its top and Fernhollow at its foot. A branch train on the branch panel, the pair's Ashgrove–Wending run on the main panel; a call at Wending on the panel of the leg it belongs to. Each service a line in its car's colour with its number at its start: passenger solid, empty long-dashed, shunt dotted, a coupled portion fine-dashed beside its train; the key along the foot. Over a running day: a wide translucent band in the car's colour where each car has actually been, sampled every five seconds; a brass line at the clock with the time under it; a dot on it for each car's present place.

The working timetable. Every service as a row: number in its car's colour, kind, car, the calls written AG 1 06:00 → WD 1 06:07 · 06:12 → CW 1 06:21, and notes such as "coupled in 1 to WD" or "joins 24 on arrival". Over a running day the actual arrival and departure from the boxes' registers follow the booked times in brass brackets.

The crew diagrams. A row per driver (name, car, on, off); a block per turn labelled with the train number in the car's colour; ridden portions hatched ("rides in 1", "rides in 24"); ticks for booking on and off; the brass clock line over a running day.

The stock working. A row per car with its dawn stabling ("AG shed road 1 (coupled)") and a block per service: passenger trains solid, empties and shunts hatched.

With a "why": a timetable is read by the line, by the person and by the vehicle; three drawings on paper disagreed within a week; one set of data and four leaves read from it do not.

§ 10 Glossary Entries added

Why: three new words for the view.

Train graph: the time–distance diagram drawn for the whole day and kept alive; the first leaf of the timetable view. Timetable view: the Traffic Office's four leaves on the weekday working, as the game shows them; over a running day the day as run beside the day as booked. As run: what actually happened, set beside what was booked: the wide band and the dot on the graph, the brass times in the working timetable.
