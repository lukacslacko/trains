§ 7.5 Gradient posts New section

Why: the Wend valley climbs from Ashgrove to Wending, and every change of grade now carries a post; the Works needs the drawing.

A new section between the hectometre posts and the baton, with Fig. 7.5, reading:

Where the grade of the line changes, a gradient post says so. It is a Slate post 1.8 m to the top with a small Ivory disc, 150 mm across with an Ink rim 12 mm wide, and two Ink arms 400 mm long and 40 mm wide, one along the line each way. Each arm is tilted with the grade on its side and lettered above with that grade in per mille, in Barlow Condensed 600, figures 100 mm high; a level stretch is lettered L and its arm lies flat. The tilt is a fixed 12° for any grade that is not level, rising or falling as the line does, so that the sign reads at a glance from a moving cab; the figure, not the angle, gives the value. Gradient posts are information and nothing more: they are not signals and carry no instruction. Book P keeps the profile they mark, and Book S says how a driver reads them.

Fig. 7.5: the gradient post at km 0.5 seen from the post side, level behind on the Ashgrove side, 12‰ rising ahead in the Down direction, with the arm length, arm width, disc and figure height dimensioned in brass, and the note "1.8 m to rail · tilt 12°".

A gradient post stands at every change of grade and nowhere else, on the post side of the line, the same side as the hectometre posts, with its arms parallel to the track so that it reads from either direction; where a change of grade falls within a loop the post moves outward past track 2, as the hectometre posts do. The arm on the side a driver is travelling towards shows the grade ahead: tilted up and away from the driver it rises, down and away it falls. The Works cuts the arms to one pattern and sets the tilt with a gauge; a post is never re-tilted to a site's true slope, and a grade that the profile does not show gets no post.

The definition list gains: Gradient post. Slate post, Ivory disc with an Ink rim, two Ink arms tilted with the grade on their side and lettered with it in per mille, L for level. One at every change of grade.

Rule I 30 Signage Rule amended

Why: the rule must place the gradient post and forbid the one liberty a site would take with it; and the advance board's distance is now a warning distance, which Rule R 10 sets by the approach, rather than a fixed 200 m.

Make name boards, plates, boards, posts and batons to the drawings in this chapter. Hectometre and kilometre posts stand at every hundred metres and every kilometre without exception, and are the only lineside plates that carry a distance. Every speed board that lowers the limit has an advance speed board of the same figures 200 m before it; a speed board that raises the limit has none. Do not add a sign that is not in Book S, and do not vary a drawing to suit a site; move the post instead.
