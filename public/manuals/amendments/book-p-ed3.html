Amendment Slip P-3

Book P, second edition (September 2026) → third edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a second-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The third edition follows the line to Coldwater. The line is now 6.4 km long and three stations; Wending is a through station with a platform on each loop track; the profile climbs again beyond Wending at 8‰ to 42.8 m; there are five gradient posts instead of three; the warning-distance table covers all four approaches and lists the distant signals beside the advance boards; and a new chapter describes the neutral section, Millers' Crossing and the two block sections. The chapters after it are renumbered by one. No rule was renumbered: P 10, P 12 and P 14 stand.

§ 2 The line Wording changed · Table rows added

Why: the line was extended from Wending to Coldwater, and Wending became a through station.

One line, two stations, three kilometres and a little more. […] The Ashgrove–Wending line runs 3.3 km south to north up the Wend valley, from the market town at the foot of the valley to the mill village by the weir. […] Kilometrage counts from the buffer stop at Ashgrove, km 0.000, to the buffer stop at Wending, km 3.300. The table listed Ashgrove (0.000–0.450), plain line (0.450–2.850) and Wending (2.850–3.300, with headshunt and buffer stop).

One line, three stations, six kilometres and a little more. The Ashgrove–Wending–Coldwater line runs 6.4 km south to north up the Wend valley, from the market town at the foot of the valley, past the mill village by the weir, to the quarry village at its head. It is electrified throughout at 1.5 kV DC by overhead line, fed from Ashgrove and from Coldwater; where the two feeds meet, at km 4.2, the line is broken by a neutral section (§ 6). Wending, once the end of the line, is now a through station with a platform on each of its loop tracks. Kilometrage counts from the buffer stop at Ashgrove, km 0.000, to the buffer stop at Coldwater, km 6.400. The table now lists Ashgrove (0.000–0.450), plain line section A (0.450–2.850), Wending (2.850–3.400, through station, platform 1 on track 1 and platform 2 on track 2 at 3.010–3.170, a stub of single line beyond each switch), plain line section B (3.400–5.950, with the neutral section and Millers' Crossing) and Coldwater (5.950–6.400, switches CW A at 6.060 and CW B at 6.320, platform at 6.110–6.270, headshunt, buffer stop at 6.400). The Duty 101 line is the same ground as far as km 3.3.

§ 3 The profile Figure changed · Wording changed

Why: Fig. 3.1 must show the whole line.

A map shows where a line goes. A profile shows how hard it is to get there. Ours climbs the valley from Ashgrove to Wending, gently, and then stops climbing. Fig. 3.1 ran from km 0 to 3.3 with three changes of grade and two stations, heights to 25.2 m.

A map shows where a line goes. A profile shows how hard it is to get there. Ours climbs the valley from Ashgrove to Wending, rests through the station, and climbs again to Coldwater. Fig. 3.1 now runs from km 0 to 6.4: level to post 0.5, rising 12‰ to 2.4, 6‰ to 2.8, level through Wending to 3.4, rising 8‰ to 5.6, level into Coldwater; heights 0, 22.8, 25.2, 25.2, 42.8 m; the three stations, the five gradient posts and a kilometre axis to 6.4. The caption notes that heights are drawn about fifteen times larger than distances and that the three level stretches are the three stations.

§ 3 The profile Table rows added · Wording changed

Why: the section table and its paragraph must reach Coldwater.

The last row read 2.800–3.300, 500 m, Level, 25.2 m. "Gradients are given in the direction of increasing kilometrage, so a positive figure rises towards Wending. Both stations stand on the level […] and Wending stands 25.2 m above Ashgrove." The note gave 12‰ as 1 in 83 and 6‰ as 1 in 167.

The rows now read 2.800–3.400, 600 m, Level, 25.2 m; 3.400–5.600, 2,200 m, Rising 8‰, 17.6 m, 42.8 m; 5.600–6.400, 800 m, Level, 42.8 m. "Gradients are given in the direction of increasing kilometrage, so a positive figure rises towards Coldwater. All three stations stand on the level […] Beyond Wending the line rests through the station limits to post 3.4, then climbs again at a steady 8‰ for 2.2 km to post 5.6, and runs level into Coldwater, 42.8 m above Ashgrove." The note adds 8‰ as 1 in 125. § 3.2 adds that the 8‰ of section B asks a little less of a train than the 12‰ of section A, and a little more of the driver descending it, since it is longer: an Up train from Coldwater is on the fall for 2.2 km before the level at Wending.

§ 4 Gradient posts Table rows added · Wording changed

Why: two more changes of grade, two more posts.

"On the ground it is written on three small posts." The reading table listed posts 0.5, 2.4 and 2.8. "A driver who knows the three posts knows the whole line."

"On the ground it is written on five small posts." The table gains post 3.4 (Wending side L, Coldwater side 8‰ rising: a Down train reads "climb begins again, 8‰", an Up train "descent over, level ahead") and post 5.6 (8‰ rising, then L: "climb over, level ahead" for a Down train, "descent begins, 8‰" for an Up train), with a note that for the posts beyond Wending the Wending side is the Up side and the Coldwater side the Down side. "A driver who knows the five posts knows the whole line."

§ 5 Speeds and warning distances Wording changed · Table changed · Note added

Why: two more station approaches, and the distant signals stand by the same rule as the advance boards.

The lead spoke of speed boards and advance boards only. The table had four rows: Up into Ashgrove (0.700, 0.450, 250 m, falling 12‰), Down into Wending (2.650, 2.850, 200 m, rising 6‰), and the two rises leaving Ashgrove and Wending.

The lead now speaks of speed boards and home signals, advance boards and distant signals. A sentence says that the same rule places the distant signals of Book S, which warn of a home signal as the advance board warns of a speed board, each one warning distance before its home by the grade of the approach. The table gains columns for the distant and the home and rows for Up into Wending (advance 3.600, board 3.400, WD 8D 3.520, WD 8 3.320, 200 m, falling 8‰ then level: under 10‰), Down into Coldwater (5.750, 5.950, CW 1D 5.780, CW 1 5.980, 200 m, level from post 5.6), and the rises leaving Wending Down (3.400) and Coldwater Up (5.950); the Ashgrove and Wending rows now show AG 2D 0.670 with AG 2 0.420 and WD 1D 2.680 with WD 1 2.880. A note explains that the Up approach to Wending falls at 8‰ for 2.2 km, long but not steep, so the ordinary 200 m applies: the extra fifty metres are given for steepness, not length.

§ 6 Features of the line New chapter

Why: the extension brought three things that are neither track nor signal and are the Engineer's to keep.

6.1 The neutral section. The overhead line is fed from Ashgrove and from Coldwater; where the feeds meet, at km 4.2, the contact wire is broken by an insulated length between 4.190 and 4.210 carrying no volts. A train that draws current across the gap arcs at the pantograph, burns the wire and trips the substation. Section boards (black square, white bar across) stand 90 m before the gap in each direction, at 4.100 for Down and 4.300 for Up trains; resume boards (white bar along the line) 90 m beyond, at 4.300 for Down and 4.100 for Up trains. The driver's conduct is Book D's; the gap, its insulators and the 90 m are the Engineer's. A Why box explains the 90 m as six or seven seconds at 50 km/h, and the placing of the gap on the 8‰ climb. 6.2 Millers' Crossing. At km 4.700 the farm track from Millers' crosses on the level: gates, cattle grids, timber decking flush with the rail head, culverts under the decking. No signals of its own; whistle boards (black W on an ivory disc) 200 m before it in each direction, at 4.500 for Down and 4.900 for Up trains, at which one long blast is sounded (Rule R 18); 200 m is the sighting distance kept clear of hedge and stack. 6.3 The block sections. Section A, Ashgrove to Wending, from AG 2 at 0.420 to WD 1 at 2.880; section B, Wending to Coldwater, from WD 8 at 3.320 to CW 1 at 5.980; one train at a time under the Line Warrant of Book S; to the Engineer a length handed to the boxes clear and taken back clear, with possessions for a later edition. A table sums up the four features, their kilometres, their marks and whose rule governs each.

§ 7 Looking after the line Renumbered · List item added

Why: the new § 6 moves this chapter to § 7, and the extension adds two things that wear.

§ 6 Looking after the line. The list of what wears ended with the fastenings.

§ 7 Looking after the line. The list gains: "The crossing and the gap. The decking at Millers' Crossing wears under hooves and tyres and its culverts choke; the insulators of the neutral section collect soot from every pantograph that passes and must be wiped before they track."

§ 8 Glossary Renumbered · Glossary

Why: the glossary moves to § 8 and gains the three features.

§ 7 Glossary, ending with "Gradient post".

§ 8 Glossary, with three new entries. Neutral section — An insulated gap in the overhead line where two feeds meet, carrying no volts; at km 4.190–4.210, marked by section and resume boards. Crossing — A place where a road or track crosses the line on the level; Millers' Crossing at km 4.700, warned by whistle boards. Block section — The single line between two boxes, home signal to home signal, into which one train at a time is admitted under a Line Warrant.
