§ 7.3 Boards New paragraph

Why: the two speed shapes must be read as a pair, and their placement fixed.

The two speed shapes are two tenses of the same word. The disc says now: the limit on it applies from the board. The triangle says ahead: the limit on it applies at a speed board one warning distance further on, 200 m on this line, and the driver has that distance in which to come down to it. Only reductions are announced; a higher limit gets a disc and nothing before it. An advance speed board stands on the same side of the line as the disc it announces, facing the trains that will meet them both, so that a driver reads triangle then disc, always in that order and never one without the other. In fog or snow the triangle is the one that matters, because the disc may not be seen in time.

§ 9.4 Editions and amendments Wording changed

Why: the first edition described pasted correction slips between editions; the Railway now issues a new edition for every change, with a slip that lists the changes, which is what you are reading.

A Book is reissued as a new edition when it changes substantially. Between editions it is corrected by amendment slips: small Ivory slips, numbered, naming the Book, the section and the page, which the holder pastes in at the place indicated. A Book with its slips in is current; a Book without is not, and the colophon says how many slips it should carry.

A Book is reissued as a new edition whenever its text or its figures change; a change to the shared stylesheet alone is not an edition. Every new edition is accompanied by an amendment slip, numbered by Book letter and edition, so that Amendment Slip I-2 takes Book I from its first edition to its second. The slip lists every change in Book order: the section, a line on why, and the wording before and after, or the new text in full. It is a small Book page in its own right, kept with the Book at amendments/book-i-ed2.html, linked from the Book's colophon and from the Library, and linking back to the sections it amends. A holder of the previous edition reads the slip, or pastes it inside the back cover, and is current; the colophon of every edition names the slips that lead to it.
