The sixth edition opens the Fernhollow branch at the junction switch WD J and brings in the two things a branch needs from a driver: multiple working of two Class 1 cars, and the splitting and joining of a pair at Wending under the Box's signals and a call-on. Three sections are new, § 4.11 to § 4.13, with two new rules, D 26 and D 28; the Class 1 handbook gains a note on working in multiple; a duty sheet is added for Duty 401; the junction moves the north end of Wending, and the posts, the distants and the Duty 301 narrative follow it; seven terms join the glossary. Nothing is renumbered and Rules D 10 to D 24 are unchanged.

§ 4.11 The branch New section

A second line, with its own kilometrage, speed and grade, and a junction to read.

Beyond Wending's north stub, at the junction switch WD J at 3.36, the line divides. Straight on is the main line to Coldwater. Diverging, to the left as you face Down, the Fernhollow branch curves away into a side valley and climbs to Fernhollow, a hamlet with a chapel, 2.3 kilometres from the junction. The branch has its own kilometrage, counted from WD J, and its posts are lettered to say so: F 0.1, F 0.2 and so on to F 2.3. Down on the branch is away from Wending; Up is towards it. Everything you know applies, with three differences, set out in a table: line speed 40 km/h against 50 (the boards at F 0.14 and F 2.0 read 40 on the running side; notch 2 holds it on the climb); rising 10‰ from F 0.3 to F 1.9 against the main line's 8‰ (gradient posts at F 0.3 and F 1.9; Up trains descend 10‰ to the junction, so WD 10D and the advance board stand the longer 250 m out, at F 0.33 and F 0.39); and Fernhollow a single platform track with no loop, against Coldwater's loop and headshunt (arrive under FH 1D and FH 1, stop at the board at F 2.255, change ends, and leave on FH 2 and Mr Thorne's baton the way you came).

Which way WD J will send you is the Box's decision and the switch's fact. The message that clears WD 7 or WD 9 says which: switch WD J lies straight for Coldwater, lies for the branch for Fernhollow. Read the indicator at the junction as you approach it (§ 4.6): the bar along the track means Coldwater, the bar leaning away means Fernhollow. If it disagrees with the message, stop short of the switch and speak to the Box. Coming back, the two lines converge at WD J under two homes, WD 8 for the main line and WD 10 for the branch; whichever you arrive under, the north stub beyond the junction is shared with every movement leaving Wending northward, and the Box's route locks it for one movement at a time (Rule S 32). A home at Stop at the junction usually means somebody is on the stub ahead of you.

Why we do this: The branch is slower and steeper than the main line because it is a side valley and was built for a hamlet: forty is what the curves allow and ten in a thousand is what the ground gave. It has its own kilometrage because a post that read 4.7 on the branch would be a lie about Millers' Crossing. The letter on the plate is there so that "stopped at 0.7" and "stopped at F 0.7" can never be mistaken for each other.

§ 4.12 Multiple working New section · Rule D 26

Two Larks make one train; the driver must know what follows the leading cab and what does not.

Two Class 1 cars couple to each other as any two vehicles do, coupler on contact and pipe and control line by hand (§ 6). Once the control line is connected the pair is one train, driven from one cab: the controller in the leading cab works both cars' motors, the brake handle works both cars' brakes, the doors key works every door, and the lights the leading cab calls for appear at the ends of the train, head at the front, tail at the rear, nothing at the coupled ends between. This is multiple working.

Rule D 26 Multiple working. Two Class 1 cars coupled with pipe and control line connected work as one train from one cab, both pantographs up, both cars' motors answering the controller; the other car's cabs show the lights the leading cab calls for, head at the leading end, tail at the trailing end, nothing at coupled ends. Prove the brake after every coupling.

The other car needs a driver in it only to be prepared: both pantographs go up, both parking brakes come off, and then its cabs are left alone; a colleague may ride in it. From the leading cab the pair behaves like a heavier Lark: notch 3 up the 12‰ as notch 2 takes one car; from 50 km/h a Full application stops it in the same hundred metres; the train is 44 metres long, which at a stop board is 22 metres more to bring to rest exactly.

Why we do this: One driver, one handle, one train: the control line exists so that a two-car train has one will. The rear car's cabs follow the leading cab's lights for the same reason the coach's lamps do, so that the train shows what it is from both ends without anybody walking. The brake is proved after every coupling because a control line can be connected and a pipe forgotten, and the proof is the only way to know.

Note: Which cab leads is not always the one you expect. A pair that arrived at a terminus with car 1004 leading leaves it with car 1001 leading, because the train has turned round and the cars have not. Before you drive, look at which car stands at the leading end. If it is the other one, walk to it: its cab is now yours.

§ 4.13 Splitting and joining New section · Rule D 28

The two operations a branch makes of a pair at Wending, each a coupling operation of § 6 done under the Box's signals.

A pair from Ashgrove is one train as far as Wending and two trains beyond it: one car to Coldwater on the main line, the other to Fernhollow on the branch. Coming back, the two cars meet at Wending from opposite valleys and become one train again for Ashgrove. The first is a split and the second a join.

Rule D 28 Splitting and joining. To split: stop at the board, secure the cab, uncouple at the coupling (pipe first, then the coupler), set TAIL on the new rear cab of your portion, and return to your cab; the other portion's driver takes their cab. To join: the standing portion waits with its train brake applied; the arriving car is called on at shunting speed and makes contact at 2 km/h or less; its driver connects the pipe and control line; the train is proved from the leading cab before it leaves. Portions leave a station in the order they stand: the one nearest the exit first.

Splitting at Wending, in six steps: arrive on platform 1 under WD 1 and stop at the board at 3.165 with the whole pair on the platform; secure the cab (Rule R 22) and leave it; walk to the coupling and uncouple, pipe then coupler (§ 6.2, Rule D 12); enter the cab that was coupled, now your portion's rear, and set its lights to Tail; walk back to your driving cab, while the colleague in the other car takes theirs; wait for WD 7 and Mr Pell's baton, shown by car number, and read WD J's indicator, straight for Coldwater; your colleague follows two minutes later with WD 7 cleared again and WD J lying for the branch, which the Box will not clear until you are past the junction and the route is released.

Joining at Wending, in five steps: arrive on platform 2 under WD 8, stop at the board at 3.015, brake Full and leave it there; the Box gives Fernhollow line clear and clears WD 10 on its subsidiary, a call-on (Rule S 30), and the car comes onto platform 2 behind you at shunting speed, prepared to stop short of you; it makes contact at 2 km/h or less and couples (Rule D 10), and your colleague connects the pipe and control line, the pipe lamp going from parted to through; prove the brake from your cab (§ 6.3, Rule D 14), for until the proof reaches the far end of the other car the baton stays in Mr Pell's pocket; wait for WD 4 and the baton and take the pair home, its other car's cabs following yours.

The order portions leave. At a split the portion nearest the exit goes first, because it is in the way of the other. Which car that is changes each time the pair turns round at a terminus: on Duty 401 your car leads out of Ashgrove and goes first at Wending, but the other pair, having turned at Ashgrove, arrives at Wending with its branch car at the Down end, and that car leaves first, for Fernhollow, before its partner can leave for Coldwater. The working timetable in Book T is written knowing this.

Why we do this: A train that is split is two trains with one timetable path each, and a train that is joined is one path made of two. Splitting lets a single car serve a hamlet the whole pair could not fill, and joining lets both come home as one train through the busiest section of the line. The call-on exists because a join is the one occasion on which a train is sent, on purpose, into a platform that already has a train in it; it is done under a subsidiary at shunting speed so that the whole procedure is a coupling and nothing more.

Lights: Head and tail lamps at each end, switched from that end's cab; in multiple, the other car's lamps follow the leading cab.

Multiple working (new entry): Any two Larks couple and work as one train from one cab, § 4.12. The class was built for it: the control line runs through the coupler.

§ 8.3 In multiple (new): Coupled to another Lark with the control line connected, the car does whatever the leading cab tells it: its motors take the notch, its brakes take the handle, its doors take the key, and its lamps take the lights switch, head or tail according to which end of the train it is. Its own cabs are dark and its own controls are dead until it is uncoupled, when they wake at once, with the reverser in Neutral, the controller at 0 and the brake in Full, exactly as a secured cab is left. Both pantographs stay up throughout: the car takes its own power and lends its own motors. On the 12‰ a pair asks 9 kN of gravity and has 90 kN to answer it with; on the branch's 10‰ a single car climbs with 45 kN against 3.7. Nothing about the Lark changes in multiple except that there is twice as much of it.

§ 12 Glossary Glossary

Seven terms added and two amended.

Down, Up: Down is the direction of increasing kilometrage, Ashgrove towards Wending and Coldwater. Up is the opposite.

Subsidiary: Two small white lamps beside the head of some main signals, WD 4 and WD 7. Lit diagonally with the main at Stop they show Shunt: a shunting move may pass at shunting speed as far as the route goes; a train may not. Book S, Rule S 10.

Branch
The Fernhollow branch: the line that leaves the main line at the junction switch WD J and climbs to Fernhollow, with its own kilometrage lettered F, 40 km/h line speed and a 10‰ climb. § 4.11.
Call-on
A home signal's subsidiary lit for a train: the platform ahead is occupied; pass at shunting speed prepared to stop short of the vehicles, and couple to them only as the Box has instructed. Used for a join. § 4.13, Rule S 30.
Down, Up
Down is the direction of increasing kilometrage, Ashgrove towards Wending and Coldwater, and on the branch away from Wending towards Fernhollow. Up is the opposite.
Join
The making of one train from two portions at a station: the second is called on behind the first, couples, connects, and the whole is proved from the leading cab. § 4.13, Rule D 28.
Junction
Where two lines meet at a switch: at Wending, WD J at 3.36, straight for Coldwater and diverging for Fernhollow, read by its switch indicator and the Box's message. § 4.11.
Multiple working
Two Class 1 cars coupled with pipe and control line connected, driven as one train from one cab, both pantographs up, the other car's lamps following. § 4.12, Rule D 26.
Portion
One of the trains a split makes, or one of the two that a join makes one of. The portion nearest the exit leaves first.
Split
The making of two trains from one at a station: secure, uncouple, Tail on the new rear cab, back to the cab; each portion then leaves under its own signal and baton. § 4.13, Rule D 28.
Subsidiary
Two small white lamps beside the head of some main signals. At the starters WD 4 and WD 7, lit diagonally with the main at Stop they show Shunt: a shunting move may pass at shunting speed as far as the route goes; a train may not. At the homes WD 8 and WD 10 the same lamps are a call-on for a train. Book S, Rules S 10 and S 30.
