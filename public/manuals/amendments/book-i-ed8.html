Amendment Slip I-8

Book I, seventh edition (September 2026) → eighth edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a seventh-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

Five entries, in Book order. Occasion: the signal and board drawings of this Book had drifted from those of Book S. Book S's drawings are the reference for every sign; this Book now repeats them with dimensions. At the same time the subsidiary moves from beside the head of its main signal to the post directly under it.

§ 7.3 Distant signals and subsidiaries Wording changed

Why: this Book described the chevron plate as a V pointing down, while Book S § 2.5 has it point up, and Book S is right. The subsidiary is now fixed on the post under the head, so that a subsidiary beside the head can no longer break the outline of the structure (Book S, eighth edition). Two more signals have carried a subsidiary since the Fernhollow branch opened.

… with an Ivory chevron plate behind the head: a V pointing down, 600 mm across and 400 mm deep, with an Ink rim 40 mm wide. The chevron is the shape that says this signal warns, it does not stop you …

A subsidiary is a small Ink box, 300 × 200 mm, fixed beside the head of a main signal at the level of its lower lamp, with two White lamps 60 mm across set in a diagonal rising to the right. … Only the signals that Book S names carry one; on this line, WD 4 and WD 7.

… with an Ivory chevron plate behind the head: a plate 600 mm across and 800 mm high, its sides upright and its top rising to a point 400 mm above the shoulders, with an Ink rim 20 mm wide. The chevron, point up, is the shape that says this signal warns, it does not stop you …

A subsidiary is a small Ink box, 300 × 200 mm, fixed on the post directly under the head of a main signal, between the head and the name plate, so that head and subsidiary read as one signal on one structure, with two White lamps 60 mm across set in a diagonal rising to the right. … Only the signals that Book S names carry one; on the Railway, WD 4, WD 7, WD 8 and WD 10.

Why: the stop board and the Limit of Shunt board in Fig. 7.5 did not match Book S Fig. 4.1, which is the reference. The other five boards already agreed and are unchanged.

Fig. 7.5: a tall stop board with the bar across its upper half and the station name inside the board beneath it; a Limit of Shunt board with the red band filling the board and the words on a strip along its foot. Caption: "… All on Slate posts, board centres 2.0 m above rail."

Definition list: Stop board. Ivory board with an Ink border, a thick Ink bar across the upper half, the station name beneath in Barlow Condensed 600. Limit of Shunt. Ivory board with an Ink border and a Signal Red band rising diagonally to the right; the words LIMIT OF SHUNT on an Ivory strip along the foot.

Fig. 7.5: the stop board and the Limit of Shunt board copied from Book S Fig. 4.1 in shape, proportion and colour, at the size of this figure's row, with dimensions added in brass: the stop board 700 × 500 with its bar 550 × 100 and a separate name plate 800 × 180 on the post beneath it; the Limit of Shunt board 800 × 500 with a band 160 wide rising to the right and a name plate 900 × 180 beneath it. Caption: "… drawn as Book S draws them in Figs. 4.1 and 4.4. All on Slate posts, board centres 2.0 m above rail; dimensions in millimetres, in brass."

Definition list: Stop board. Ivory board 700 × 500 mm with an Ink border and a thick Ink bar 550 × 100 mm across its middle; beneath it on the post, an Ivory name plate 800 × 180 mm with an Ink border and the station name in Ink, Barlow Condensed 700. Limit of Shunt. Ivory board 800 × 500 mm with an Ink border and a Signal Red band 160 mm wide rising diagonally to the right, from the lower left corner to the upper right; beneath it on the post, an Ivory name plate 900 × 180 mm with an Ink border and the words LIMIT OF SHUNT in Ink, Barlow Condensed 700.

Why: so that the next time two Books disagree about a sign, everyone knows which one to correct.
