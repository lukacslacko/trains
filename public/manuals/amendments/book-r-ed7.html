Book R, sixth edition (September 2026) → seventh edition (September 2026). Issued 9 September 2026. Paste inside the back cover of a sixth-edition copy, or read it here to bring yourself up to date.

The seventh edition opens the signaller's chair. A Box may now be worked by a signaller in place of its station master, and on two new duties, 501 and 502, that signaller is the reader: the crossing and the junction are run again from Wending Box, every car in a colleague's hands. Book R takes the role and its authority, the fourth colleague who drives car 1002 on those duties, the two duties in the timetable chapter, and three Incident Book entries that belong to the chair rather than the cab. The working of a Box, with Rules S 34 to S 40, is in Book S's new chapter, Working a box. No rule of this Book changes its text or its number.

§ 2 Terms and directions Glossary

A duty is no longer only a driver's.

Duty. A day's work for a driver and a vehicle, laid out in the working timetable under a number: Duty 101, Duty 201, Duty 301.

Duty. A day's work for a driver and a vehicle, or for a signaller and a Box, laid out in the working timetable under a number: Duties 101 to 401 are driven, Duties 501 and 502 are worked from Wending Box.

§ 3.2 The station master Wording changed · Paragraph added

A fourth colleague drives car 1002 when it is not the reader's, and the Box may be worked by a signaller: the chapter on authority has to say whose authority that is.

"Three colleagues drive the other cars of the working timetable at the date of this edition: R. Farrow (car 1003), E. Hale (car 1004) and W. Penrose (car 1001). They keep the same Book you do."

"Four colleagues drive the other cars of the working timetable at the date of this edition: R. Farrow (car 1003), E. Hale (car 1004), W. Penrose (car 1001) and D. Corry (car 1002, on the duties where it is not yours). They keep the same Book you do."

Then a new paragraph: "A Box may be worked by a signaller on duty in place of its station master, and on Duties 501 and 502 that signaller is you. The chair carries the whole of the station master's authority within station limits and nothing more: the frame and its routes, the exchange of line clear with the neighbouring Boxes and the warrants that come of it, the baton. A Box worked this way signs its messages with the station's name alone, '— Wending', and the neighbouring Boxes address it exactly as they would the station master. The drivers on the line owe the chair what they owe the station master, and the chair owes them a cleared road. How a Box is worked, from the first 'Is line clear?' to the last warrant cancelled, is Book S, Working a box."

§ 9.1 The working timetable Wording added

The two box duties run timetables the chapter already prints.

After the Duty 401 table and its note on train 33: "Duties 501 and 502 run these two timetables again, the crossing and the junction, with every car in a colleague's hands and the player in Wending Box; the trains keep their numbers and their times, and the Box keeps them (Book S, Working a box)."

§ 10.2 The entries Table rows added · Wording added

Three things can go wrong in the chair that cannot go wrong in a cab, and each is written in the same words every time.

Three rows inserted before "Collision": Warrant cancelled early, "A warrant was cancelled before its train had arrived complete (S 36)"; Train held at the home, "A train stood at a home signal for a minute and a half waiting for the road (S 38)"; Baton shown late, "The baton was shown more than a minute after the booked time to a train that stood ready (S 40)".

The paragraph on colleagues' entries gains: "The last three entries in the table are the Box's: they are written against whoever is in the chair, and on Duties 501 and 502 that is you, while the drivers' entries stay the drivers'."

§ 11 The rules in one page Rows added

The card carries every rule number the Books use; the four rules of the chair join it after S 32.

S 34, "Line clear is given in words, only with the section clear and no warrant out; the warrant is issued with it."; S 36, "Cancel a warrant when its train has arrived complete and stands, and not before."; S 38, "Set the home before the train reaches it; take back a route you will not use by replacing its signal."; S 40, "From the Box: baton at the booked time, starter cleared, doors closed; back as the train leaves."
